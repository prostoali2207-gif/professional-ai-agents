# Instructional protocol

Use this reference when deciding the next teaching move.

## Step 1 — Determine the failure class

Ask: what prevents the next successful independent action?

- `NO_MODEL` — learner does not know what good performance consists of.
- `CUE_MISS` — relevant signal is present but unnoticed.
- `RULE_ERROR` — learner notices cue but selects wrong rule/action.
- `EXECUTION_ERROR` — decision is right, implementation is weak.
- `RETRIEVAL_ERROR` — learner can do it only with model/help visible.
- `TRANSFER_ERROR` — learner succeeds only in familiar surface conditions.
- `OVERLOAD` — too many novel interacting elements.
- `DOMAIN_GAP` — task-specific knowledge is missing.

Do not choose an intervention before classifying the likely failure.

## Step 2 — Choose minimum sufficient support

### NO_MODEL
Give:
- task purpose;
- one decision rule;
- one tiny worked contrast;
- guided attempt.

### CUE_MISS
Make the cue temporarily salient, then fade the cue.

### RULE_ERROR
Contrast two similar cases that require different decisions.

### EXECUTION_ERROR
Keep the rule stable and isolate implementation practice.

### RETRIEVAL_ERROR
Hide/reduce the model and require reconstruction before reopening it.

### TRANSFER_ERROR
Change one contextual feature at a time; ask learner to identify what remains invariant.

### OVERLOAD
Reduce interacting novelty while preserving the core decision; rebuild complexity progressively.

### DOMAIN_GAP
Retrieve/use the domain authority. Do not patch with generic pedagogy.

## Worked example design

A tiny model should expose:
`CUE -> DECISION -> WHY -> ACTION`

Prefer:
- one authentic fragment;
- visible decision;
- short contrast with a plausible wrong move.

Avoid:
- full polished task;
- unexplained answer;
- example so simplified that the real cue disappears.

## Feedback resolution

Use the narrowest feedback that changes the next attempt.

Default:
- KEEP: one behavior already working;
- FIX: one dominant bottleneck;
- RULE: the decision rule;
- RETRY: immediate application.

These labels are optional. The logic is required; the surface format is not.

## Fading ladder

Possible sequence:
A. coach models;
B. learner completes part of the decision;
C. learner decides with a cue;
D. learner decides without cue;
E. learner self-checks before feedback;
F. learner transfers to changed context;
G. coach audits exceptions only.

Do not advance by time. Advance by evidence.

## Stopping rule

End a teaching segment when the learner can perform the active target independently at the current difficulty and another immediate drill has low information value.

Do not keep generating exercises merely to fill a lesson.
