# Supplement evidence reference

Version: 0.1
Reviewed: 2026-09-22.
Primary evidence anchor: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements exercise/athletic-performance fact sheet plus the local nutrition evidence package.

Use this module only after the user's target outcome and relevant safety context are known.

## Decision rule

Do not recommend a supplement because it is popular, natural, mechanistically plausible, or associated with a biomarker change. Ask:
1. Does human outcome evidence support the user's actual goal?
2. Is the effect large/reliable enough to matter?
3. Is the active ingredient and dose transparent?
4. Is there a simpler food/training solution?
5. Does medication, disease, pregnancy, age, sleep, stimulant exposure or tested-sport status change the decision?

## Creatine

Preferred form: creatine monohydrate.

For a generally healthy adult doing resistance training:
- simple default: 3–5 g/day;
- loading is optional, not required;
- if rapid saturation is specifically desired, a common evidence-based loading protocol is 20 g/day divided into four 5 g doses for 5–7 days, then 3–5 g/day;
- an alternative is about 3–6 g/day without loading for several weeks.

Expected value:
- strongest supplement evidence for repeated high-intensity exercise capacity and support of strength/lean-mass training adaptations;
- does not replace progressive resistance training or sufficient food/protein.

Common practical issue:
- body mass can rise from water retention; do not label this automatically as fat gain.

Do not present expensive alternate creatine forms as superior without evidence.

Escalate/verify before personalized use when renal disease, pregnancy, complex medication use or another material medical condition is present.

## Protein powder

Treat as a convenient protein food, not a special anabolic pharmacology category.

If daily high-quality protein is already sufficient, extra powder is not automatically useful. Route target protein/energy decisions to the nutrition skill.

Useful when:
- food volume/appetite makes protein difficult;
- convenience or timing matters;
- the powder replaces a less convenient protein source.

## Caffeine

Evidence supports acute performance benefit in some activities, but response varies.

Authoritative evidence commonly studies about 2–6 mg/kg before exercise. Do not treat the top of this range as a target.

Practical rule:
- use the lowest dose that produces the desired effect;
- account for all caffeine from coffee, energy drinks, pre-workouts, pills and medications;
- more is unlikely to provide proportional benefit and increases insomnia, restlessness, nausea, tachycardia/arrhythmia and anxiety risk;
- sleep impairment can erase training/recovery value.

The FDA notes that about 400 mg/day for healthy adults is not generally associated with dangerous effects; this is not a performance target or a personalized safety guarantee.

Avoid recommending concentrated/pure caffeine powder.

## Beta-alanine

Useful context:
- can increase muscle carnosine;
- evidence is most relevant to repeated/high-intensity work lasting roughly 1–4 minutes, not ordinary hypertrophy itself.

A common evidence-supported regimen is 4–6 g/day in divided doses for at least 2–4 weeks.

Main common adverse effect:
- paresthesia/tingling, reduced by smaller divided doses or sustained-release forms.

For a user whose only goal is muscle mass from normal resistance training, classify as OPTIONAL rather than core.

## BCAA

If the user already consumes adequate high-quality protein:
- usually LOW_VALUE;
- evidence does not consistently show extra hypertrophy/recovery benefit beyond sufficient complete protein.

Do not sell leucine/BCAA as a substitute for adequate daily protein and energy.

## Citrulline / citrulline malate

Evidence for acute exercise benefit is limited and inconsistent across heterogeneous studies.

For hypertrophy:
- do not claim meaningful direct muscle gain;
- classify as OPTIONAL at best unless a specific performance outcome and evidence justify it.

Do not infer the amount of elemental citrulline from a branded malate ratio unless the label is clear.

## HMB

Evidence is mixed and context-dependent. Do not make it a default hypertrophy supplement for a healthy trained adult.

Potential value is more plausible in selected high-stress, detrained, older or damage-heavy contexts, but context-specific evidence should be checked before recommending.

## Vitamins/minerals

Do not use megadoses as generic performance enhancers.

Use when:
- a dietary gap/deficiency is plausible or documented;
- a clinician has indicated a need;
- the specific nutrient has a supported use in the actual context.

For iron, vitamin D and other clinically meaningful deficiencies, avoid diagnosing from symptoms alone.

## Pre-workouts

Never evaluate a pre-workout as one undifferentiated product.

Parse:
- caffeine/stimulants;
- beta-alanine;
- citrulline;
- creatine;
- electrolytes;
- vitamins;
- proprietary/novel ingredients.

Then assess each ingredient and the total exposure.

A proprietary blend that hides doses is a product-quality uncertainty, not a reason to assume an effective dose.

## “Test boosters”, prohormone-like and grey-market products

Require exact ingredient identity.

Do not infer hypertrophy benefit from a small or transient hormone change.

If the product contains a drug-like, SARM-like, prohormone or undisclosed active:
- reclassify out of ordinary supplement coaching;
- use the pharmacology-risk and anti-doping modules;
- verify current regulatory status live.

## Mass gainers

Treat as a calorie/protein convenience product.

Route the actual calorie/protein decision to low-appetite-muscle-gain-nutrition. Evaluate only product-specific ingredient/quality issues here.

## Minimal stack principle

For a mass/strength-focused healthy adult:
- fix training, energy and protein first;
- creatine is usually the first evidence-supported supplement worth considering;
- caffeine is situational, not mandatory;
- add other products only when a specific outcome justifies them.

Prefer one change at a time when feasible so effect/tolerance can be observed.
