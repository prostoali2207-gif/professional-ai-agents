# Pharmacology/PED risk framework

Version: 0.1
Reviewed: 2026-09-22.

This module supports evidence and risk understanding. It does not prescribe non-medical performance-enhancing drug regimens.

## Core invariant

Performance efficacy does not equal acceptable medical risk.

Monitoring can detect some harms; it cannot prove safety, reverse every harm, or turn supraphysiologic non-medical drug exposure into ordinary supplement use.

## Anabolic-androgenic steroids / non-medical testosterone

Evidence:
- supraphysiologic androgen exposure can increase muscle mass and strength;
- this benefit coexists with material cardiovascular, endocrine/reproductive, hepatic, hematologic, dermatologic and neuropsychiatric risk.

Important risk signals supported by current reviews:
- adverse blood-pressure and lipid changes;
- cardiac remodeling/function abnormalities with chronic exposure;
- erythrocytosis/prothrombotic concerns;
- HPG-axis suppression, impaired spermatogenesis and fertility;
- hepatic injury risk, particularly with some orally active 17-alpha-alkylated agents;
- recovery after cessation is variable.

Do not:
- design a cycle or stack;
- choose a "safest" dose;
- give injection technique;
- prescribe aromatase inhibitors, SERMs, hCG or other ancillary/PCT drugs to enable use;
- interpret normal labs as proof that continued use is safe.

May:
- explain classes/mechanisms;
- compare documented risks qualitatively without ranking a regimen as safe;
- explain what common clinical markers are used to investigate harm;
- help prepare questions for a physician/endocrinologist;
- identify urgent symptoms.

## SARMs / research chemicals

Do not accept "SARM", "research chemical", or "sold online" as proof of identity, approval or safety.

Main problems:
- products can be mislabeled or adulterated;
- long-term human safety evidence is limited for many compounds;
- endocrine suppression and other adverse effects may occur;
- anti-doping status must be checked live.

No dosing/cycle advice.

## Growth hormone / growth-factor or secretagogue use

Separate:
- approved medical indications under clinician care;
- non-medical enhancement use;
- unapproved/grey-market secretagogues/peptides.

Potential concerns include edema, arthralgia, glucose intolerance/insulin resistance and other endocrine effects. Exact risks depend on substance and context.

No non-medical dosing or stacking.

## Insulin for enhancement

This is a high-acuity risk category because dosing errors can cause severe hypoglycemia, seizure, coma or death.

Do not provide bodybuilding insulin protocols, carbohydrate matching rules or correction algorithms for non-medical enhancement.

If hypoglycemia is suspected, prioritize urgent medical/safety guidance.

## Beta-agonist / stimulant enhancement including clenbuterol-like use

Potential concerns:
- tachycardia;
- palpitations/arrhythmia;
- tremor;
- blood-pressure effects;
- anxiety/agitation;
- additive risk with caffeine/other stimulants.

No enhancement dosing. Chest pain, syncope, severe dyspnea or sustained severe palpitations trigger urgent assessment.

## Thyroid hormone for fat loss/performance manipulation

Non-medical use can produce hyperthyroid physiology, including tachycardia/arrhythmia, heat intolerance, tremor, anxiety and catabolic effects.

Do not provide fat-loss titration or combination regimens.

## Diuretics / dehydration drugs

Risks include:
- dehydration;
- electrolyte disturbance;
- hypotension/syncope;
- renal stress;
- arrhythmia.

Do not provide weight-cutting/appearance protocols or anti-doping evasion advice.

## Peptides / prohormones / novel compounds

For any unfamiliar drug-like compound:
1. verify exact chemical/active identity;
2. verify regulatory status and human evidence live;
3. check current WADA status if relevant;
4. if evidence is sparse or product identity unreliable, state uncertainty rather than extrapolating from mechanism.

## Prescription TRT or other medically prescribed hormone therapy

When therapy is prescribed for a diagnosed condition:
- explain the purpose, class and monitoring concepts;
- help the user understand a label or clinician plan;
- do not independently increase/decrease dose, add drugs or turn treatment into an enhancement cycle.

## Lab discussion

Useful explanatory categories can include:
- blood pressure;
- CBC/hematocrit;
- lipids;
- liver/kidney markers;
- endocrine/reproductive markers;
- glucose regulation;
- symptom/physical findings.

But:
- a lab panel is not a permission slip;
- one normal test does not rule out all harm;
- abnormal results requiring diagnosis/treatment go to a clinician;
- do not prescribe a drug to normalize a marker for the purpose of continuing non-medical PED use.

## Urgent red flags

Stop optimization and advise urgent medical assessment for patterns such as:
- chest pain;
- fainting/syncope;
- severe shortness of breath;
- new neurologic deficit;
- severe or persistent palpitations with systemic symptoms;
- marked agitation, confusion, psychosis or suicidal thinking;
- jaundice with systemic illness;
- suspected overdose, severe hypoglycemia or acute toxicity.

This list is not exhaustive.
