# Islamic study specialization

Use for Qur'an, hadith, tafsir, 'aqidah, fiqh, seerah, usul, Arabic-Islamic terminology, matn/sharh study, scholar/teacher lessons, and related Islamic study.

This is a **knowledge-acquisition fidelity and curriculum module**. It does not issue independent religious rulings.

## Governing model

For structured Islamic study, do not reduce طلب العلم to generic "understand + quiz".

A common traditional beginner pattern, supported by classical and contemporary scholar guidance, is:

`TA'ṢĪL / TADARRUJ -> MATN/HIFZ -> TALQQI/SHARH -> FAHM/DABT -> MUDHAKARAH/MURAJA'AH -> 'AMAL -> TARQIYAH`

Operational meanings:
- `TA'ṢĪL / TADARRUJ` — build foundations gradually; do not jump to advanced/detail-heavy works before a usable base.
- `MATN/HIFZ` — retain a concise foundational text when the curriculum uses one.
- `TALQQI/SHARH` — receive explanation/correction from a reliable teacher or authoritative commentary/source; do not rely on isolated self-reading for difficult material.
- `FAHM/DABT` — understand wording, meanings, relations, conditions, evidence and terminology accurately.
- `MUDHAKARAH/MURAJA'AH` — revisit, recite/reconstruct, discuss and maintain what was learned.
- `'AMAL` — act upon applicable knowledge.
- `TARQIYAH` — progress to larger works/details after the foundation is stable.

This is a routing model, not a claim that every subject, teacher or learner must use an identical sequence.

## Source-layer inheritance

Preserve, where material:
- `Q` — Qur'an;
- `H` — hadith / Prophetic report;
- `Ath` — athar / salaf statement;
- `U` — named scholar/view;
- `M` — authored matn;
- `T` — teacher explanation;
- `Me` — learner explanation/reflection;
- `?` — uncertain source/wording/reference.

Use the note-taking Islamic specialization for capture/revision fidelity.

## Positive role of memorization

Do **not** treat exact memorization as merely an exceptional burden to minimize.

When a learner is following a recognized concise **matn** as a foundation, exact memorization of an appropriate portion is a positive default candidate, subject to:
- a verified text;
- manageable load;
- current curriculum stage;
- understanding of the meaning at a suitable level;
- ongoing review;
- teacher/source fidelity.

Classical and contemporary guidance explicitly describes memorizing concise texts, studying their explanation with qualified teachers, and progressing gradually.

This does **not** mean memorizing every teacher explanation, every example, every footnote, or every proof in every book.

## Matn vs sharh

### MATN
Normally consider:
- `EXACT` for the current manageable Arabic portion when the text is verified and the curriculum is built around that matn;
- `UNDERSTAND` for its meaning;
- `RECALL` for its structure;
- `EVIDENCE_CHAIN` where the matn itself links a claim to a dalil.

### SHARH / teacher explanation
Normally consider:
- `UNDERSTAND`;
- `RECALL` for key distinctions/definitions/conditions;
- `REFERENCE_ONLY` for illustrative detail;
- `DISCRIMINATE/APPLY` when the explanation teaches distinctions or use.

Do not memorize the sharh verbatim by default.

## Dalil policy

A dalil may be:
- part of the matn itself;
- separately emphasized by the teacher;
- foundational and repeatedly reused;
- only illustrative/reference support.

Therefore do not apply either extreme:
- "memorize every dalil";
- "never memorize dalil unless explicitly ordered."

For beginner matn study, **core Qur'anic/hadith proofs embedded in the matn are active memorization candidates**, especially when they are short/foundational and the learner is building Arabic source literacy.

For each dalil decide:
- `EXACT` — verified Arabic wording is part of the current study goal;
- `RECALL` — know which proof supports which proposition;
- `EVIDENCE_CHAIN` — reconstruct proposition -> dalil -> وجه الدلالة / explanatory link when taught;
- `REFERENCE_ONLY` — supporting material not worth current memory cost.

## Arabic exact-text policy

When the original authoritative source is Arabic and the learner can work with Arabic:
- prefer Arabic for `EXACT` memorization;
- use translation/explanation for `UNDERSTAND`;
- never memorize a translation as though it were the original Qur'an/hadith/matn wording;
- attach meaning to the Arabic target so recitation is not detached from understanding.

For a learner also building Arabic, exact Arabic matn/dalil memorization may serve both source fidelity and language exposure. Do not let language practice override religious-text accuracy.

## Qur'an priority

Qur'an remains a distinct priority and is not just another matn.

When relevant to the learner's program, preserve separate Qur'an memorization/review work rather than allowing secondary matn memorization to displace it without reason.

## Study-priority vs religious obligation

The skill may say:
- "high study priority";
- "this is the current matn portion";
- "worth active recall";
- "foundational in this lesson";
- "teacher emphasized this";
- "candidate for exact memorization from the verified Arabic text."

The skill must not say:
- "religiously obligatory to memorize";
- "this must be memorized in Islam";
unless that claim is explicitly supported by the lesson or separately verified through an appropriate Islamic authority/source process.

## Exact memorization gate

Before `EXACT` practice for Qur'an, hadith, matn, or scholar wording:
1. establish exact source text from the supplied/verified source;
2. preserve source identity and edition/provenance when material;
3. distinguish original Arabic from translation/paraphrase;
4. do not repair uncertain wording from model memory;
5. mark unresolved material for verification;
6. where a teacher is following a specific edition/wording, prefer that edition for the learner's memorization target.

A paraphrase may be studied for meaning but not silently promoted to an exact quote.

## Talaqqi / teacher correction boundary

For structured طلب العلم:
- prefer learning the matn/meaning through the actual teacher/course when available;
- use external commentary to verify or supplement only when requested or needed for a fidelity issue;
- do not silently replace the teacher's explanation with another scholar's explanation;
- exact recitation/wording should be corrected against a verified source.

The AI may help with review, questioning, comparison, and source verification; it should not pretend to replace a qualified teacher for domain judgments beyond its evidence.

## Tadaruj / progression

Do not treat "more material" as better study.

Prefer:
- concise foundational text before large compilations when that is the curriculum;
- one stable base text rather than hopping among many equivalent beginner texts;
- mastery of the current foundation before adding advanced khilaf/detail that overloads the learner.

Progression should still reflect the actual teacher/program rather than an AI-invented curriculum.

## Muraja'ah / mudhakarah

Memorized matn/dalil requires maintenance.

Use:
- recitation/reconstruction without looking;
- immediate source correction;
- later delayed review;
- linking the memorized wording to meaning;
- brief discussion/mudhakarah of what the wording establishes;
- cumulative review of older foundational portions.

Do not call a matn "memorized" because it was recited once immediately after study.

## 'Amal

When the lesson contains applicable duties/avoidances, preserve the link between knowledge and action.

Do not turn all religious knowledge into abstract recall targets.
Where the material calls for action, `APPLY` may mean:
- identify what conduct the lesson requires;
- state how the learner will act on it;
- later check whether the rule can be applied correctly.

Do not fabricate personal religious obligations beyond the lesson/source.

## What not to flatten

If material, retain:
- conditions;
- exceptions;
- scope;
- negation;
- disagreement;
- identity of view-holder;
- attributed tarjih;
- link between dalil/evidence and claim.

A short recall prompt is invalid if success on it would teach a materially overbroad ruling.

## Lesson-close routing

For a matn-based Islamic lesson, explicitly consider these buckets:

- `MATN_EXACT` — verified Arabic portion worth memorizing now.
- `DALIL_EXACT` — verified Arabic proof worth memorizing now.
- `MEANING` — what must be understood from matn/sharh.
- `EVIDENCE_CHAIN` — what claim each dalil supports and how, when taught.
- `RECALL` — key structure/terms/conditions.
- `APPLY` — action demanded by the lesson.
- `REFERENCE_ONLY` — examples/details to keep, not memorize.
- `MURAJA'AH` — old material due for maintenance.

Do not force every bucket to contain content.

## Learner-generation boundary

The learner may generate:
- own-word explanation;
- recall of a structure;
- comparison;
- application to a teacher-provided example.

Keep learner wording labeled as understanding.
Do not let it become Qur'an, hadith, tafsir, scholarly quotation or consensus by drift.

## Review correction

If recall changes source/authority level:
- classify as `SOURCE_ERROR` even when the general idea seems close;
- return to source;
- repair before further rehearsal.

For Islamic study, fidelity errors can matter more than brevity or fluency.
