# Islamic lesson note-taking specialization

Use this module whenever the lesson is Islamic knowledge: Qur'an/tafsir, hadith, fiqh, 'aqidah, seerah, usul, Arabic-Islamic terminology, or a scholar's lesson.

This module extends the general note-taking coach. It does not replace the core `CAPTURE -> REVISE -> RETRIEVE` workflow.

## Governing principle

In Islamic notes, compression must never erase **who said what**, **what the evidence is**, or **how certain the attribution is**.

The learner should be able to look back later and distinguish revealed text, transmitted reports, scholarly explanation, teacher commentary, and their own understanding.

## Source-layer tags

Use a small, stable tag set:

- `Q:` Qur'an — surah:ayah when known.
- `H:` hadith — source/reference and grading when actually known.
- `Ath:` athar / statement of a Companion or early scholar.
- `U:` named scholar's statement/opinion.
- `T:` teacher's explanation, synthesis, example, or preference.
- `Me:` learner's own wording, reflection, question, or tentative inference.
- `?` attribution/reference/wording needs verification.

Do not over-tag every sentence. Tag when source identity matters or could later be confused.

## Exactness hierarchy

### Preserve exactly or verify before quoting

Prefer exact capture for:
- Qur'anic wording;
- hadith wording when recorded as a quotation;
- formal definitions whose wording is being taught;
- named conditions, pillars, nullifiers, exceptions, or legal distinctions when wording materially changes the ruling;
- direct statements attributed to a scholar when quotation accuracy matters.

If exact wording is not secure:
- do not reconstruct Arabic from memory;
- do not put a paraphrase inside quotation marks;
- write `meaning:`, `summary:`, or `?` and verify later.

### Compress explanation

Teacher explanations, repeated examples, transitions, and elaboration can usually be compressed, provided the compression preserves:
- the ruling/claim;
- its condition or boundary;
- its evidence;
- who is responsible for the conclusion.

## Attribution discipline

Never silently convert:
- teacher explanation -> "Islam says";
- one scholar's statement -> consensus;
- teacher's tarjih -> undisputed ruling;
- learner inference -> scholar's statement;
- paraphrase -> quotation;
- an unverified hadith/reference -> authenticated report.

When the teacher says a hadith is sahih/da'if or attributes a view to a scholar, record that as the teacher's attribution unless independently verified.

## Uncertainty protocol

When unsure, capture uncertainty instead of inventing precision:

- `H: ? source`
- `U: Ibn Taymiyyah? verify`
- `Q: 2:? verify ayah`
- `T: appears to mean ... ?`

During revision, resolve only material uncertainties from reliable sources. Leave unresolved items explicitly marked.

## Domain routing

### Tafsir

Default structure:

`Ayah -> key vocabulary -> overall meaning -> tafsir points -> supporting evidence/context -> benefits/lessons`

Keep separate:
- transmitted tafsir / scholar explanation;
- teacher's explanation;
- learner's personal tadabbur/reflection.

Do not label personal reflection as tafsir.

### Hadith

Default structure:

`Hadith/reference -> authenticity status if known -> important wording -> vocabulary -> meaning -> benefits/rulings -> qualifications/exceptions`

If the lesson discusses isnad, narrator criticism, variants, or grading reasons, preserve those as their own layer rather than blending them into the matn.

Do not invent a grading.

### Fiqh

Default structure:

`Mas'alah -> ruling -> conditions/pillars/nullifiers/exceptions -> evidence -> known disagreement -> evidence/reasoning per view -> tarjih + whose tarjih`

For disagreement:
- represent views fairly enough to distinguish them;
- attach evidence/reasoning to the correct view;
- record who prefers which view;
- do not convert the learner's preference into the lesson's conclusion.

### 'Aqidah

Default structure:

`term/issue -> definition -> evidence -> distinction/boundary -> common confusion or opposing claim -> response -> conditions/exceptions`

Do not overcompress conditions and distinctions merely to make the page shorter, especially where the teacher is distinguishing related terms or explaining conditions/barriers.

### Seerah / history

Default structure:

`event -> actors -> sequence -> source certainty/date if material -> consequence -> lessons explicitly drawn by teacher/scholar`

Separate established event details from interpretive lessons. Do not invent motives for historical figures.

### Usul / terminology

Default structure:

`term -> exact/working definition -> components/conditions -> example -> counterexample -> consequence`

Terminology is often cumulative; preserve distinctions that later rulings depend on.

## Evidence-chain layout

When a lesson is argument-heavy, use:

`QUESTION -> CLAIM/RULING -> DALIL -> HOW DALIL SUPPORTS CLAIM -> CONDITION/EXCEPTION -> DISAGREEMENT -> TARJIH (attributed)`

This is often more useful than a generic outline because it preserves the chain of reasoning.

## Anti-verbatim adaptation for Islamic lessons

Do **not** solve over-writing by deleting dalil, attribution, conditions, or scholarly disagreement.

Compress in this order:
1. rhetorical repetition;
2. duplicate examples;
3. long teacher phrasing;
4. obvious transitions;
5. repeated restatement of an already captured point.

Preserve:
1. Qur'an/hadith reference and exact text when needed;
2. the mas'alah;
3. the ruling/claim;
4. conditions/exceptions;
5. dalil;
6. attribution;
7. material disagreement;
8. reasoning that explains why the evidence applies.

## Review checklist

After revision ask:

1. Can I distinguish `Q/H/Ath/U/T/Me` where it matters?
2. Did I accidentally turn a paraphrase into a quote?
3. Is every material hadith/scholar attribution either sourced or marked for verification?
4. Did I preserve conditions/exceptions?
5. If there is disagreement, can I reconstruct each view and who preferred what?
6. Can I explain the issue without adding claims that are not in the lesson?
7. What must I verify before teaching/sharing these notes?

## Retrieval for Islamic study

Use retrieval questions such as:
- What is the mas'alah?
- What is the ruling/claim?
- What is the dalil?
- How was the dalil connected to the claim?
- What conditions/exceptions were mentioned?
- Was there khilaf? What were the views?
- Whose tarjih was recorded?
- Which part is my own understanding rather than transmitted knowledge?

The aim is not merely memorizing a page; it is reconstructing the lesson faithfully.

## Sharing boundary

Private rough notes may contain `?` and incomplete references.

Before sharing, teaching, posting, or attributing:
- verify material Qur'an/hadith references;
- verify quotations and scholar attributions when possible;
- preserve uncertainty rather than presenting it as certainty;
- distinguish quotation from paraphrase.

## Religious evidence underpinning the specialization

- Qur'an 17:36: do not follow/say that of which one has no knowledge.
- Zayd ibn Thabit's report: the Prophet ﷺ supplicated for the one who hears a hadith, preserves it, and conveys it; Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2656, graded hasan by al-Tirmidhi.
- Muhammad ibn Sirin: "Indeed this knowledge is religion, so look from whom you take your religion." Recorded in the Introduction to Sahih Muslim.
- The severe warning against deliberately lying about the Prophet ﷺ is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari 110 and Sahih Muslim 3.

These sources support source-care and attribution discipline; they do not imply that every explanatory note must be verbatim.
