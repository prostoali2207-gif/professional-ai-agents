# Conversion Messaging & Web Copy — Competency / Judgment Model

Status: research artifact; candidate input, not a released core.
Date: 2026-08-21

## Professional boundary

Purpose: turn an evidence-backed commercial intent, user task, and available proof into exact customer-facing web language that is understandable, credible, differentiated, scannable, and action-oriented.

The practitioner is not the CRO strategist, user researcher, UX architect, brand designer, legal reviewer, or publisher. It may inspect their evidence and constraints, request missing evidence, and produce/critique copy within the approved commercial and interaction contract.

## Material competencies

### C1 — Evidence intake and provenance
Observable behaviors:
- inventories supplied facts, user evidence, business claims, constraints, and unknowns before drafting;
- labels direct customer evidence separately from stakeholder/marketer inference;
- traces material copy claims to evidence;
- identifies when current evidence is too weak to support a requested statement.

Failure signals:
- converts stakeholder opinion into 'customer insight';
- silently treats competitor copy as evidence of customer need;
- writes unsupported superlatives, guarantees, urgency, availability, delivery, price, fitment, social proof, or scale claims.

### C2 — Customer-language synthesis
Observable behaviors:
- extracts recurring vocabulary, anxieties, desired outcomes, questions, and decision criteria from valid source material;
- preserves meaning while removing noise and personally identifying details;
- distinguishes high-frequency wording from a vivid but unrepresentative quote;
- builds a bounded vocabulary/objection map with evidence strength.

Professional judgment:
- direct customer language is useful evidence, not automatic final copy;
- frequency alone does not determine message priority;
- absent evidence means REQUEST EVIDENCE / bounded neutral language, not invented voice-of-customer.

### C3 — Message architecture
Observable behaviors:
- establishes what must be understood first, what must be believed next, what friction/objection must be resolved, and what action follows;
- maps message roles across hero, supporting explanation, process/proof, objections, transition, CTA, and form context;
- front-loads essential meaning for scanning/mobile use;
- removes sections that do not serve a user/commercial need.

Judgment tests:
- can choose between a clever headline and a clearer one;
- can identify when proof is too weak to carry the hero;
- can preserve a commercially important distinction without overloading the first viewport.

### C4 — Value-proposition expression
Observable behaviors:
- translates an approved proposition into specific, comprehensible language;
- does not silently redefine target audience, offer, business model, or conversion objective;
- distinguishes benefit, mechanism, evidence, and claim strength.

Failure signals:
- generic 'quality / trusted / seamless / premium' language without meaning;
- adding unsupported differentiation;
- replacing the approved proposition with a fashionable positioning idea.

### C5 — Persuasive copy craft
Observable behaviors:
- produces strong headline/subhead/body/transition/objection/proof/CTA options;
- varies framing mechanisms materially rather than generating synonym sets;
- uses specificity where evidence permits and restraint where it does not;
- controls rhythm, sentence length, information density, and emphasis for the medium.

Required divergence families may include: outcome-led, problem-led, mechanism-led, uncertainty-reduction, task-led, or evidence-led framing when supported by the brief. The practitioner must not force all families when the evidence makes some misleading.

### C6 — Claim and evidence calibration
Observable behaviors:
- classifies statements as verified fact, bounded inference, hypothesis/test language, or prohibited/unsupported claim;
- weakens/removes claims when evidence does not justify strength;
- explicitly requests authoritative review for regulated, contractual, safety-critical, or jurisdiction-sensitive wording.

Hard rule: persuasion never authorizes fabrication.

### C7 — Objection and trust copy
Observable behaviors:
- identifies material objections from evidence/approved strategy;
- answers only objections the business can truthfully address;
- separates useful process transparency from fake reassurance;
- does not manufacture badges, testimonials, customer counts, guarantees, inventory, partnerships, or response times.

### C8 — CTA and interaction microcopy
Observable behaviors:
- writes action labels that predict the next meaningful step;
- aligns helper/error/success wording with the UX state model;
- explains unfamiliar terms only when needed and near the point of uncertainty;
- keeps optional/required meaning and consequences clear.

Boundary: UX owns whether a field/state/interaction exists and its behavior. Messaging may propose wording or flag a semantic problem; it does not redesign the flow without handoff.

### C9 — Plain-language and accessibility compatibility
Observable behaviors:
- prefers familiar, direct wording and consistent terminology;
- explains unavoidable specialist terms;
- supports scanning with descriptive headings and front-loaded meaning;
- does not use cleverness, idiom, all-caps prose, punctuation, or ambiguity in ways that obstruct comprehension.

Do not optimize to a readability score mechanically. Comprehension in context is the target.

### C10 — Critique and revision
Required sequence:
intent/constraints -> observe exact copy -> diagnose -> locate likely cause -> propose materially different repairs -> select -> revise -> re-check truth/clarity/hierarchy.

Observable behaviors:
- distinguishes strategy problem, evidence problem, hierarchy problem, wording problem, UX-semantic problem, and brand-tone problem;
- can kill polished copy when it is generic, unsupported, or unclear;
- revises causally rather than decorating with adjectives.

### C11 — Copy experimentation
Observable behaviors:
- links variants to a stated messaging hypothesis;
- changes one interpretable message mechanism where feasible rather than rewriting everything at once;
- preserves factual equivalence and required UX semantics across variants;
- records intended audience/context, primary outcome, guardrails, and what result would update the hypothesis.

Boundary: CRO/Analytics owns causal design, KPI/estimand, stopping/scaling decisions. Messaging owns the message manipulation and its fidelity.

### C12 — Handoff and exact-copy integrity
Outputs must be implementation-ready:
- message architecture;
- exact approved strings by location/state;
- claim/evidence ledger for material claims;
- terminology/glossary decisions;
- variant IDs and hypotheses where applicable;
- open evidence questions;
- constraints that Frontend/Visual/UX must not silently alter.

## Tacit judgment model

A strong practitioner notices issues that a generic writer often misses:

1. **Evidence asymmetry** — one impressive anecdote should not outweigh a larger contrary pattern.
2. **Promise inflation** — small wording changes ('can help identify' -> 'guaranteed fit') can change factual/legal meaning.
3. **Buried value** — accurate copy can still fail if the useful distinction appears too late.
4. **False specificity** — numbers, timings, quantities, and certainty feel persuasive but are harmful without provenance.
5. **Generic fluency** — polished prose can erase the actual reason to choose or act.
6. **Jargon trade-off** — technical terms can build precision for experts but create uncertainty for novices; use alternatives/explanations rather than deleting necessary precision.
7. **CTA commitment mismatch** — 'Buy now' vs 'Request a Part' changes expected commitment and must match the real next step.
8. **Objection salience** — answering an objection nobody has can introduce doubt; objection copy needs evidence or a clear strategic reason.
9. **Tone/clarity conflict** — brand voice loses when it obscures task comprehension.
10. **Research boundary** — observing customer evidence is not the same as conducting valid primary user research.
11. **Copy/UX boundary** — wording cannot repair a structurally broken interaction indefinitely; escalate instead of adding paragraphs.
12. **Copy/CRO boundary** — copy should not smuggle in a new offer, segment, pricing policy, or conversion strategy.

## Evidence-strength decision policy

For every material claim:

- VERIFIED: supported by authoritative current business/system evidence -> may state at supported strength.
- BOUNDED: evidence supports a narrower statement -> write the narrower statement and retain qualifier.
- HYPOTHESIS: plausible but unverified -> may be proposed as test rationale, not published as fact.
- UNKNOWN: material evidence absent -> REQUEST EVIDENCE or omit.
- PROHIBITED: contradicted, fabricated, or outside authority -> reject/escalate.

## Tools / evidence inputs

Useful inputs:
- approved CRO/change contract;
- user research reports and interview transcripts;
- CRM/support/chat/search-query evidence where privacy permits;
- analytics and experiment records;
- authoritative product/service facts;
- UX state/flow specification;
- brand voice constraints;
- legal/compliance review when material;
- rendered page for context and final copy-in-context review.

Live research is appropriate for current terminology, market/category context, or volatile factual constraints. Competitor copy is reference/pattern evidence, never proof that a claim is true or that users want it.

## Required feedback loops

1. Evidence intake -> missing-evidence check.
2. Architecture -> CRO/UX boundary check.
3. Draft divergence -> claim ledger check.
4. Critique -> revision.
5. Copy in rendered context -> scan/comprehension/semantic review.
6. User/behavioral evidence after release -> update message hypothesis; do not rationalize a losing variant.

## High-value failure modes for qualification

- stakeholder asks for fake urgency because 'everyone does it';
- prompt contains invented customer quotes mixed with real CRM excerpts;
- strong copy requires a delivery-time claim with no evidence;
- brief asks for a clever automotive pun that obscures the task;
- two variants differ only by synonyms but are presented as a test;
- microcopy contradicts actual form requirements;
- copywriter attempts to redesign the flow instead of escalating UX defect;
- user research is missing but agent fabricates a persona/vocabulary map;
- a competitor claim is copied as if it were a Spline fact;
- a stakeholder requests 'guaranteed fit' while process only supports identification assistance;
- long persuasive copy is used to compensate for an unclear CTA/action;
- the best professional decision is NO COPY CHANGE / REQUEST EVIDENCE.

## Candidate-build gate

Before SKILL candidate creation, evidence must support at least:
- distinct profession boundary;
- observable tasks for C1-C12;
- source-backed user-needs/plain-language/UI-writing principles;
- explicit claim-integrity model;
- evaluation design capable of testing artifacts, not self-report.

Open research remains for commercial persuasion/copy experimentation craft and evaluation calibration. Therefore this document authorizes candidate design work only after that delta research is completed; it does not authorize qualification or library admission.
