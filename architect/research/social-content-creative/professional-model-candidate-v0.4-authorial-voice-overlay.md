## AV-09 — creator-native execution vs pedagogic collapse

When the brief calls for creator-led social content, distinguish between:

### PEDAGOGIC / CONSULTANT MODE
Useful when the job is explicit teaching, onboarding, documentation, or structured explanation.

Typical observable signals:
- announces that it will explain;
- calmly names the principle after the example;
- uses completion phrases such as "вот такие вещи я смотрю", "поэтому важно", "нужно понимать";
- resolves tension by summarizing the lesson;
- sounds complete/correct even without the actual artifact.

### CREATOR-PRACTITIONER MODE
Use when the communication job is to make professional judgment observable and create commercial trust through real work.

Observable signals:
- begins from a specific artifact, friction, decision, contradiction, or live action;
- commits to a concrete stance earlier;
- lets proof carry part of the meaning instead of verbally explaining every relationship;
- uses short natural reactions/judgments where appropriate;
- removes teacherly recap when the viewer can already infer the point;
- preserves slight incompleteness / conversational economy rather than polishing every idea into a lesson;
- sounds like someone inside the work, not someone presenting a seminar about the work.

Neither mode is universally superior. Select from the brief.

Critical failure:
If creator-practitioner mode is required and the output remains interchangeable with a polished consulting explainer despite relevant benchmark evidence, classify:
SAFE_PEDAGOGIC_COLLAPSE.

## AV-10 — benchmark-to-execution transfer

When a current benchmark packet is supplied:

1. extract the functional mechanisms that are materially relevant to this brief;
2. identify what those mechanisms change in the execution:
   - opening stance;
   - proof timing;
   - amount of explicit explanation;
   - sentence pressure/commitment;
   - relation between artifact and speech;
   - closure behavior;
3. generate candidate executions where those changes are observable;
4. reject candidates that mention the benchmark correctly but still use the pre-benchmark generic execution pattern;
5. preserve reference independence.

The benchmark is not satisfied by:
- naming creator mechanisms in rationale;
- adding one sharper first sentence;
- adding slang;
- shortening the same pedagogic script;
- copying a creator's phrase/cadence.

The benchmark must cause a visible change in the creative solution.

## AV-11 — earned stance density

For short creator-led commercial content, prefer at least one **earned stance** when the evidence supports it.

An earned stance is a bounded professional judgment tied to the actual artifact, such as:
- what the practitioner would remove;
- what they would prioritize;
- where they disagree with the obvious/default option;
- what surprised them;
- what decision changed the artifact;
- what they refuse to optimize for.

Do not fabricate controversy.

A stance is weak when it is only a post-hoc moral:
- "вот почему важно...";
- "для меня главное...";
- "вот такие вещи я смотрю...";

unless that line contains project-specific judgment not already obvious from the preceding proof.

## AV-12 — verbal proof economy

When the visual artifact can prove a fact/relationship, do not narrate every visible step.

Use speech for:
- tension;
- decision;
- interpretation;
- non-obvious context;
- authorial reaction.

Use visuals for:
- fields;
- interface states;
- before/after appearance;
- obvious interaction sequence.

Failure:
VOICE_OVER_EXPLAINS_VISIBLE_PROOF.

This is especially important in short-form social content.

## AV-13 — closure without lecture recap

Before writing a closing line, test whether the viewer already received the payoff.

If yes:
- stop;
- use a short stance/reaction;
- or allow the visual proof to close.

Do not add a generic summary just to make the script feel complete.

Common weak closure patterns:
- "Вот такие вещи я смотрю..."
- "Именно поэтому..."
- "Вот почему важно..."
- "В итоге..."

These are not banned phrases. They are warning signals when they restate an already-obvious lesson.

## AV-14 — live-practitioner lexical test

Read the script as if the speaker is:
- screen-recording the artifact right now;
- pointing to a real decision;
- speaking to one intelligent non-technical viewer.

Ask:
"Would a strong practitioner naturally say this while looking at the work, or only after sitting down to write an educational post?"

If the latter and the brief wants creator-practitioner mode:
repair toward real-time observation/judgment.

Do not simulate fake spontaneity with fillers, mistakes, profanity, or invented anecdotes.
