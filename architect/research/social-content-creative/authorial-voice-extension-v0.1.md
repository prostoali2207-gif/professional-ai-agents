## 1. Target work

Extend the existing Social Content Creative profession with a reusable capability for creating distinctive, speakable, authorial public-facing scripts without changing upstream strategy, facts, experiment locks or release authority.

Target output remains the same professional object:
- exact social script/copy and creative execution inside an approved brief.

This is therefore a delta to the existing profession, not a new top-level profession.

## 2. Observed failure

Applied work can satisfy the current qualified controls and still fail perceptually:

- truthful;
- structurally coherent;
- relevant;
- shootable;
- non-misleading;
- but generic, polished, interchangeable and recognizably "AI-written".

The failure is material because the creator's spoken identity can itself become part of the content experience, particularly in faceless narration.

Do not encode "engaging", "charismatic" or "funny" as unexplained taste labels. The extension must expose observable craft decisions and failure modes.

## 6. Evidence seed and transfer limits

### EV-AV-01 — Humor as a contextual executional device
Martin Eisend, "A Meta-Analysis of Humor in Advertising", Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science 37(2), 191–203.
DOI: 10.1007/s11747-008-0096-y.

Supports:
- humor can affect attention, positive affect and attitude toward the ad;
- humor effects vary with audience/stimulus/product conditions;
- credibility effects can be negative.

Decision implication:
The core must choose whether/where humor helps the communication job. It must not implement joke density or "always be funny" as a rule.

Transfer limit:
Advertising evidence does not establish exact organic-social retention/conversion effects.

### EV-AV-02 — Conversational wording changes processing
Richard E. Mayer, Sherry Fennell, Lindsay Farmer, Julie Campbell, "A Personalization Effect in Multimedia Learning", Journal of Educational Psychology 96(2), 389–395.
DOI: 10.1037/0022-0663.96.2.389.

Supports:
- conversational wording can materially change audience processing in narrated multimedia;
- direct-address/personalized language can be functionally meaningful, not cosmetic.

Transfer limit:
Educational transfer-test effects do not establish social-platform growth effects.

### EV-AV-03 — Lexical content and acoustic delivery are separable
Andrew Rosenberg & Julia Hirschberg, "Charisma perception from text and speech", Speech Communication 51(7), 640–655.
DOI: 10.1016/j.specom.2008.11.001.

Supports:
- charisma judgments can be studied from both lexical/syntactic and acoustic/prosodic features;
- text-level authorial craft and performed delivery should not be collapsed into one competency.

Decision implication:
This EXTEND track owns script/persona craft. Physical vocal-performance direction remains a separate Architect question.

### EV-AV-04 — Profanity is context-dependent, not an authenticity shortcut
David S. Lie, Billy Sung, Michelle Stankovic, Felix Septianto, "How profanity influences perceived authenticity and perceived helpfulness of online reviews", Decision Support Systems 178 (2024), 114144.
DOI: 10.1016/j.dss.2023.114144.

Supports:
- profanity can interact with subjectivity and perceived authenticity/helpfulness in one online-review context.

Transfer limit:
Does not justify profanity for creator content generally; no default profanity rule.

## 7. Candidate competency model for delta research

### AV-01 Authorial voice/persona construction
Observable behavior:
- constructs a bounded voice contract from approved brand/language context;
- defines stance, viewer relationship, lexical range, recurring devices and anti-patterns;
- distinguishes durable identity from one-off novelty;
- does not fabricate biography/personality claims.

Expert discriminator:
A strong practitioner creates recognizability without reducing the voice to catchphrases or slang tokens.

### AV-02 Spoken-language writing
Observable behavior:
- produces scripts that remain natural when read aloud;
- controls clause length, syntactic variation, repetition, direct address and emphasis opportunities;
- removes page-prose artifacts and over-formal transitions.

Boundary:
Does not claim to coach the actual speaker's prosody/diction unless separately qualified.

### AV-03 Humor mechanism judgment
Observable behavior:
- selects or rejects humor based on communication function;
- can use contrast, incongruity, misdirection, self-deprecation, callback, escalation, register collision or restraint;
- explains why the mechanism fits this beat.

Failure:
"Add joke every N seconds" or one universal humor formula.

### AV-04 Humor/proof integration
Observable behavior:
- keeps the factual/proof-bearing clause unambiguous;
- places comic beats where they do not distort claims or instructions;
- can remove a funny line when it weakens credibility/comprehension.

### AV-05 Persona continuity
Observable behavior:
- maintains recognizable voice across a sequence of scripts;
- avoids repeated boilerplate, catchphrase spam, manufactured slang and persona drift.

### AV-06 Anti-template diagnosis
Observable behavior:
- identifies generic creator boilerplate, empty intensifiers, synthetic transitions and faux-conversational filler;
- repairs the causal source of genericness rather than adding random slang.

### AV-07 Reference abstraction / anti-imitation
Observable behavior:
- decomposes references into mechanism/function;
- explicitly marks non-transferable expression;
- generates materially different execution.

### AV-08 Controlled profanity decision
Observable behavior:
- treats profanity as optional;
- compares profane/non-profane alternatives when material;
- rejects profanity used only as a shortcut to "authenticity";
- preserves audience/project constraints.

## 8. Hidden competency / red-team questions

Research must answer:
- What makes a voice recognizably consistent without being repetitive?
- How does a writer distinguish authored conversational language from fake "chatty" AI copy?
- Which humor mechanisms survive factual/technical explanatory content without reducing comprehension?
- How should a core critique a joke that is funny but wrong for the author's identity?
- When does self-deprecation increase relatability versus weaken competence signals?
- How do callbacks work across a series without making first-time viewers feel excluded?
- What textual cues are safely owned by Creator and what belongs to acoustic performance direction?
- How should multilingual/code-switching creators preserve persona without accidental caricature?
- How should profanity be evaluated under cultural/brand/reputation constraints?
- How can the system learn from high-performing references without overfitting to one creator?
