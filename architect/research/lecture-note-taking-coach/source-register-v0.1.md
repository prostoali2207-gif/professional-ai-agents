# Lecture Note-Taking Coach — source register v0.1

Status: PRE-SKILL EVIDENCE
Date: 2026-09-23

## Evidence policy

The skill does not claim one universally best note-taking format or medium. Evidence supports teaching active selection/organization, revision, retrieval, and metacognitive regulation, while effects depend on task and learner.

## Sources

### S1 — Dunlosky et al. (2013)
"Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques."
Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1):4-58.
DOI: 10.1177/1529100612453266

Use: practice testing/distributed practice have broad evidence; summarization is not automatically high-utility and depends strongly on learner skill/training.
Decision impact: notes should feed retrieval practice; the coach must teach summarization rather than assume novices already know how.

### S2 — Schindler & Richter (2023)
"Text Generation Benefits Learning: a Meta-Analytic Review."
Educational Psychology Review 35:44.
DOI: 10.1007/s10648-023-09758-w

Use: learner-generated text can support learning relative to passive reception.
Decision impact: preserve learner generation rather than outsourcing all summarization to AI.

### S3 — Flanigan et al. / contemporary meta-analysis (2022)
"The effect of notetaking method on academic performance: A systematic review and meta-analysis."
Contemporary Educational Psychology 68:102025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cedpsych.2021.102025

Use: under controlled conditions, longhand vs digital medium itself did not show a reliable performance difference.
Decision impact: do not teach "paper always beats laptop"; manage distraction and processing instead.

### S4 — Weinstock et al. (2021)
"Don't Ditch the Laptop Just Yet: A Direct Replication..."
Psychological Science.
PubMed PMID: 33539228

Use: replication/meta-analytic evidence does not support a simple causal claim that handwriting is superior because it reduces verbatim notes.
Decision impact: avoid medium dogma; target note behavior.

### S5 — Titsworth & Kiewra (2004)
"Spoken Organizational Lecture Cues and Student Notetaking as Facilitators of Student Learning."
Contemporary Educational Psychology 29(4):447-461.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cedpsych.2003.12.001

Use: organizational cues can increase capture of structure/details and improve learning.
Decision impact: explicitly train detection of verbal structure cues.

### S6 — Luo, Kiewra & Samuelson (2016)
"Revising Lecture Notes: How Revision, Pauses, and Partners Affect Note Taking and Achievement."
Instructional Science 44:45-67.

Use: note revision is a distinct useful stage, not merely copying.
Decision impact: architecture uses CAPTURE -> REVISE rather than treating first-pass notes as final.

### S7 — Kiewra, Colliot & Lu (2018)
"Note This: How to Improve Student Note Taking." IDEA Paper #73. ERIC ED588353.

Use: students often record incomplete notes; instruction, cues, pauses/revision, and transformed review can help.
Decision impact: teach skills explicitly and use scaffolded supports.

### S8 — Notetaking instruction study (2020)
"Effects of notetaking instruction on intermediate and advanced L2 English learners: A quasi-experimental study."
System / ScienceDirect record S1475158519306113.

Use: a scaffolded instructional cycle improved captured information units.
Decision impact: progression should be trained over repeated lessons, not explained once.

### S9 — "The mind, not the pen" (2026)
Learning and Instruction 104:102360.

Use: recent evidence reports benefits from deep note-taking instruction and challenges simplistic medium explanations.
Decision impact: prioritize cognitive strategy instruction.

### S10 — Karpicke & Blunt (2011)
"Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping."
Science 331(6018):772-775.
DOI: 10.1126/science.1199327

Use: retrieval practice is a powerful learning activity.
Decision impact: post-lesson notes should become questions/cues for recall, not only a document to reread.

## Conflicts / uncertainty

- Medium (handwriting vs typing) evidence is mixed historically; current architecture treats medium as secondary to processing, distraction, task demands, and accessibility.
- Verbatim overlap is not a universal failure: exact text may be necessary for definitions, quotations, formulas, proofs or source-critical study.
- Compression quality cannot be judged by brevity alone; downstream reconstruction/recall matters.
