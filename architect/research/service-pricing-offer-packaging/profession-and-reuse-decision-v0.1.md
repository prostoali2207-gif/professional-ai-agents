## 1. The real profession

A solo or small-studio provider of digital work (websites, CRM, automation, custom internal tools) who sells to non-technical small and medium business owners. The recurring work:

- turning a vague client problem into a bounded, sellable package;
- deciding what is included, excluded, revisable, and who supplies what (content, access, decisions);
- setting a price and its structure (fixed fee, tiers, deposit, maintenance retainer);
- presenting the price so that the buyer compares value and options, not hourly rates or other vendors;
- protecting margin when scope creeps, budgets tighten or the client asks for a discount;
- keeping the published offer consistent with what can actually be delivered by one person.

Adjacent professions this competence must not absorb: positioning and strategy, creative/content production, sales conversations (inbound conversion), contract law, tax and accounting, paid media.

## 2. Decisions that separate a strong practitioner from an average one

| Decision | Average behavior | Strong behavior (source basis) |
|---|---|---|
| What the price is anchored to | cost-plus or "what others charge" | the value of the outcome to this buyer (Enns), with a premium over the hourly estimate for risk and surprises (Stark) |
| Price presentation | one number | three options differing in ambition, risk and investment (Enns, Stark) |
| Quote type | hourly or "estimate" | fixed fee stated as a quote (Stark) |
| Scope definition | feature list | buyer job, inclusions, explicit exclusions, revision limit, client inputs, support window (productized service practice, Casel) |
| Discovery | free, unstructured | structured value conversation; paid discovery when scoping effort is large (Enns, Stark, Casel) |
| Efficiency from AI/automation | passes savings to client as discount, or hides AI use | prices outcomes, so efficiency becomes margin; explains honestly where AI speeds work and where human judgment stays (Enns) |
| Which buyer market sets the price | the seller's own market | the buyer's market and currency; the spread between markets is very large (see knowledge file) |
| Recurring revenue | none | maintenance/support retainer sized as a share of build cost or a monthly band |

## 3. Hidden competencies (not in the user's request)

1. Buyer-market and currency selection per package (decision-changing, see data spread).
2. Scope-creep control: exclusions, change-request pricing, revision limits.
3. Payment structure: deposit, milestones, what happens on non-payment (escalate legal wording to a human).
4. Floor price: the lowest price that still pays for real hours plus risk premium.
5. Evidence discipline: market bands from vendor blogs are marketing-grade; each number must carry source, date and grade.
6. Claim discipline: never promise leads, rankings or revenue without evidence (inherited from PBGS-OFFER-002).
7. Honest first-client pricing: an n=1 paid client price is an anchor, not a market price.

Result: no existing core owns offer packaging and pricing judgment. Classification: **BUILD NEW (lean candidate)**, composed with the cores above. Evidence: PBGS-PROF-GAP-004, the 2026-10-17 audit finding "no prices" after the system reached 66k words of rules, and the absence of any pricing logic in the six qualified cores.

Not a universal "business consultant" agent: boundary is limited to packaging, price structure, price level reasoning, scope and payment terms, for small digital-service providers.
