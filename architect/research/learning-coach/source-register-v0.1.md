# Learning Coach — source register v0.1

Status: PRE-SKILL EVIDENCE
Date: 2026-09-23

## Evidence policy

This capability uses evidence to constrain instructional decisions, not to prescribe one universal teaching style. Effects depend on learner prior knowledge, task structure, domain, feedback type, and implementation.

## Sources

### L1 — Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (2019)
"Cognitive Architecture and Instructional Design: 20 Years Later."
Educational Psychology Review 31.
DOI: 10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5

Use: novices can benefit from worked examples and high guidance; as expertise increases, the same guidance can become redundant, motivating guidance fading and expertise-sensitive instruction.
Decision impact: model before unsupported problem solving for a true beginner; fade examples/hints with evidence of competence.

### L2 — van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen (2010)
"Scaffolding in Teacher-Student Interaction: A Decade of Research."
Educational Psychology Review 22:271-296.
DOI: 10.1007/s10648-010-9127-6

Use: identifies contingency, fading, and transfer of responsibility as central scaffolding characteristics.
Decision impact: support should respond to learner performance and be withdrawn rather than becoming permanent.

### L3 — Brummer, de Boer, Mouw et al. (2024)
"A meta-analysis of the effects of context, content, and task factors of digitally delivered instructional feedback on learning performance."
Learning Environments Research 27:453-476.
DOI: 10.1007/s10984-024-09501-4

Use: instructional feedback has heterogeneous effects; context/content/task and feedback focus matter, with no one-size-fits-all configuration.
Decision impact: feedback is selected for the observed error and next attempt rather than emitted as a fixed rubric dump.

### L4 — Dunlosky et al. (2013)
"Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques."
Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1):4-58.
DOI: 10.1177/1529100612453266

Use: retrieval/practice testing has broad support; summarization quality depends substantially on learner skill.
Decision impact: do not equate explanation or rereading with mastery; include performance/retrieval checks.

### L5 — Karpicke & Blunt (2011)
"Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping."
Science 331(6018):772-775.
DOI: 10.1126/science.1199327

Use: retrieval is a learning event, not only an assessment event.
Decision impact: after guided learning, require the learner to reconstruct or apply without the model visible.

## Evidence limits

- Worked-example findings do not justify showing full solutions indefinitely.
- Scaffolding does not imply maximum help; contingency and transfer of responsibility are central.
- Feedback evidence does not support a single universal timing/content rule.
- Retrieval evidence does not mean every interaction must become a quiz.
- These sources constrain the generic pedagogy layer; domain-specific teaching still requires the domain model.
