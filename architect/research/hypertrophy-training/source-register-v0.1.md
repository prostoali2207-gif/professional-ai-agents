## Stable knowledge retained in the skill

- progressive resistance training improves strength and muscle size in healthy adults;
- exercise-specific strength benefits from heavier-load practice and specificity;
- hypertrophy can be achieved across a broad load spectrum; load choice is therefore also a fatigue, comfort and task-specificity decision;
- weekly hard-set exposure matters, with diminishing returns and individual tolerance;
- momentary failure is not a universal requirement; closer-to-failure work trades stimulus against fatigue;
- frequency is partly a distribution/practice variable rather than a universal hypertrophy target;
- exercise order should reflect adaptation priority;
- exercise variation should be systematic, not random;
- program decisions require longitudinal, standardized observations.

## Slow-changing / contested knowledge kept out of unconditional rules

- exact weekly-set optimum or maximum recoverable volume;
- exact "best" RIR for every exercise/population;
- universal direct/indirect set fractional weights;
- superiority of lengthened partials over full ROM across all muscles;
- fixed deload timing/duration;
- universal per-session set caps;
- one ideal rest interval;
- one ideal periodization model.

These require bounded language, context, and evidence refresh when decision-critical.

## Live/context-only knowledge

- the trainee's current exercise availability, load increments, history and technique standard;
- current symptoms, illness, recovery constraints and schedule;
- new position stands or material meta-analyses that supersede this register;
- special-population/clinical guidance outside the healthy-adult boundary.

## Evidence conflicts preserved

1. Deload: complete cessation may reduce strength adaptation in one trained sample, while reduced-volume/frequency deloads did not harm adaptations in one small untrained sample. Therefore no universal deload schedule/form is encoded.
2. Longer-muscle-length training: earlier reviews suggested advantages in some muscles; newer regional meta-analysis estimates small/trivial pooled differences. Therefore it is an exercise-specific option, not a hard rule.
3. Failure/proximity: failure is not consistently superior, but closer-to-failure sets may improve hypertrophy on average while increasing fatigue. Therefore target RIR depends on exercise, load, safety, session position and recovery.
