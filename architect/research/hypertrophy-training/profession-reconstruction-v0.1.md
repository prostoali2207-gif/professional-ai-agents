# Hypertrophy Training — profession reconstruction v0.1

Status: PRE-SKILL RESEARCH
Date: 2026-09-21
Target: applied skill `hypertrophy-training`

## Target work, not title

The requested work is not generic "fitness coaching". It is the professional work of an evidence-led **resistance-training program designer / strength-and-hypertrophy practitioner for healthy adults**, with exercise-physiology and applied-biomechanics knowledge embedded where those disciplines materially change programming decisions.

The target practitioner must convert longitudinal training evidence into concrete next-session and next-block decisions for hypertrophy and strength while minimizing fatigue that does not improve the target adaptation.

## Primary responsibilities

1. Reconstruct the trainee's current training state from history rather than treating the latest message as the whole case.
2. Separate hypertrophy objectives, exercise-specific strength objectives, constraints and competing priorities.
3. Prescribe and adjust exercise selection, order, weekly set exposure, load, repetition ranges, RIR/RPE targets, frequency and recovery spacing.
4. Progress load/repetitions/sets only when the observed trajectory justifies the change.
5. Diagnose apparent plateaus before adding work.
6. Distinguish useful training fatigue from accumulating fatigue that degrades stimulus quality or recovery.
7. Substitute exercises by training function and target, not by name similarity.
8. Preserve comparability of training observations so trends are not inferred from changed technique, ROM, equipment or exercise setup.
9. Maintain explicit state across sessions/blocks and supersede stale prescriptions when newer observations justify it.
10. Recognize safety/medical boundaries and escalate rather than diagnose.

## Professional outputs

- session prescription with exercise, order, sets, reps/range, target RIR/RPE and load-selection rule;
- weekly/microcycle distribution;
- progression/hold/regression decision for each material exercise;
- volume change decision by target muscle or lift;
- fatigue/recovery action, including criteria-based deload when justified;
- exercise substitution with preserved training function;
- plateau diagnosis with discriminating evidence;
- state update and next-observation requirements;
- explicit uncertainty / missing-data / escalation record.

## Scope classification

### CORE

- resistance-training program design for hypertrophy and exercise-specific strength;
- volume/intensity/frequency/effort manipulation;
- progression and autoregulation;
- fatigue management and recovery-aware programming;
- exercise selection, ordering, standardization and substitution;
- longitudinal trend interpretation and plateau diagnosis;
- RIR/RPE use and calibration;
- program adherence/feasibility trade-offs;
- downstream verification from subsequent training performance.

### BOUNDARY-CRITICAL

- applied exercise physiology: enough to interpret stimulus, fatigue, adaptation and specificity;
- applied biomechanics/anatomy: enough to select and substitute exercises, preserve target function, ROM and joint tolerance;
- preparticipation/symptom screening: enough to stop/escalate when ordinary programming is no longer the right authority;
- recovery context: sleep, life stress, illness, schedule and nutritional adequacy can confound performance trends, but the skill does not become a medical or nutrition specialist;
- measurement quality: technique, ROM, equipment, load calibration and RIR estimation can invalidate apparent trends.

### ESCALATION

- diagnosis/treatment of injury or disease;
- persistent or severe pain, neurological symptoms, acute trauma, unexplained cardiopulmonary symptoms or other red flags;
- rehabilitation programming requiring clinical judgment;
- medication/supplement prescribing;
- eating-disorder or clinical nutrition management;
- special-population programming when decision-critical conditions fall outside validated healthy-adult evidence.

### CONTEXTUAL

- sport-specific peaking/power;
- endurance-concurrent training;
- competition rules;
- advanced velocity-based hardware;
- local gym/equipment constraints.

### OUT OF SCOPE

- medical diagnosis;
- physiotherapy;
- general meal planning/macronutrient coaching as a primary service;
- pharmacology/PED protocols;
- claiming body-composition outcomes from training alone;
- arbitrary motivational coaching unrelated to programming decisions.

## Difficult professional decisions

### D1 — Is a bad session noise or a programming signal?

Expert behavior: compare several standardized exposures, inspect RIR/technique/ROM/equipment/recovery context, and change the program only when decision value exceeds noise.

Weak behavior: reduce or increase volume after one off day.

### D2 — Should load, repetitions or sets increase?

Expert behavior: choose the smallest variable change that targets the limiting adaptation while preserving execution quality and recoverability.

Weak behavior: apply "progressive overload" as mandatory load increase every session.

### D3 — How close to failure?

Expert behavior: treat proximity to failure as a continuum. Use sufficiently high effort for hypertrophy while recognizing that momentary failure is not universally required and carries a fatigue/recovery cost.

Weak behavior: "failure is mandatory" or "never train to failure".

### D4 — How much weekly volume?

Expert behavior: use prior tolerated workload, current progression and recovery as the individual anchor; use population evidence as a prior, not a compulsory set count; account for direct and meaningful indirect work without fake precision.

Weak behavior: assign a universal 10/20/30 sets per muscle regardless of history.

### D5 — Is frequency itself the intervention?

Expert behavior: use frequency to distribute useful volume, practice priority lifts and protect session quality/recovery; distinguish hypertrophy from exercise-specific strength.

Weak behavior: insist every muscle must be trained at one universal frequency.

### D6 — Is exercise variation useful?

Expert behavior: keep enough stability to measure progression; change exercises when target coverage, joint tolerance, equipment, motivation or regional stimulus justifies it; avoid random novelty.

Weak behavior: rotate exercises every workout or never substitute anything.

### D7 — Is this a true plateau?

Expert behavior: verify adherence, stable execution, sufficient effort, recovery, load/rep accuracy, target specificity and repeated non-progression before altering dose.

Weak behavior: diagnose plateau from a short flat sequence or chase novelty.

### D8 — Deload or continue?

Expert behavior: use accumulated-fatigue evidence and task context; reduce the fatigue-producing variables with a clear re-entry condition. Do not encode a universal calendar deload.

Weak behavior: deload every N weeks regardless of trajectory, or refuse deload until severe collapse.

### D9 — Exercise substitution

Expert behavior: preserve the adaptation function: target musculature, relevant joint actions/ROM, stability demands, strength specificity when material, loadability, proximity-to-failure practicality and symptom tolerance.

Weak behavior: substitute by surface category ("another chest exercise").

## Cues experts use

High-value cues:
- repetitions and load at a comparable RIR/RPE;
- standardized technique/ROM/equipment;
- performance trend across repeated exposures;
- ability to reproduce target musculature and stable execution;
- persistent session-to-session rep/load deterioration;
- soreness/fatigue that materially overlaps the next target exposure;
- subjective readiness in context, not as a single oracle;
- adherence and session completion;
- exercise-specific strength practice frequency;
- pain/symptom flags.

Misleading cues when used alone:
- pump;
- soreness;
- sweat;
- session tonnage;
- one exceptional or poor workout;
- bodyweight fluctuations over a few days;
- novelty;
- arbitrary PR frequency;
- "feeling destroyed" after training.

## Core trade-offs

- stimulus per set vs fatigue per set;
- volume vs recoverability;
- heavier load specificity for strength vs joint/technique/fatigue cost;
- exercise stability for measurement vs variation for target coverage/tolerance;
- close-to-failure stimulus vs fatigue and RIR error;
- specificity vs redundancy;
- more data vs low-friction logging/adherence;
- aggressive progression vs preserving high-quality comparable exposures.

## Expert-vs-average discriminators

A strong practitioner:
- changes one or few causal variables after diagnosis instead of rewriting the whole program;
- uses longitudinal evidence and preserves comparability;
- can explain what observation would reverse a decision;
- does not turn population averages into individual absolutes;
- distinguishes strength specificity from hypertrophy stimulus;
- can hold a program steady when the best decision is "no change";
- reduces unnecessary fatigue without underdosing effort;
- escalates symptoms rather than diagnosing;
- keeps evidence strength proportional to claim strength.

An average practitioner tends to:
- use fixed templates independent of training history;
- equate progression with weight-only progression;
- use failure, soreness, pump or sweat as proof of stimulus;
- add sets when progress slows before checking confounders;
- change exercises too frequently to interpret trends;
- overreact to one session;
- use calendar deloads as universal law;
- prescribe around pain without an authority boundary.

## Verification evidence

Local decision success is not established by a plausible program. Verify downstream:
- did prescribed RIR/rep targets remain feasible?
- did standardized performance improve, hold or deteriorate as expected?
- did target volume remain recoverable into the next exposure?
- did a substitution preserve progression and target function?
- did a deload restore performance/readiness without unnecessary detraining?
- did a plateau intervention produce a changed trajectory?
- were symptom/escalation boundaries respected?

## Architecture choice

Use one modular applied practitioner with staged reference/procedural resources and structured training state.

Do not split into multiple agents by default. Separate agents would share the same training history, create state-consistency risk and add coordination cost without independent evidence that the target decisions improve. Clinical/rehab and nutrition boundaries are handoffs, not subagents.

## Claim boundary

The intended initial population is healthy adults performing resistance training for hypertrophy and/or exercise-specific strength. Special populations and clinical rehabilitation require additional evidence and qualification before inclusion.
