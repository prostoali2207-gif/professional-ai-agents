# Study Strategy — source register v0.1

Status: PRE-SKILL EVIDENCE
Date: 2026-10-28

## Evidence policy

Use learning-science evidence to constrain study decisions, not to produce a universal timetable or one study method for all knowledge.

## S1 — Rowland (2014)
"The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: a meta-analytic review of the testing effect."
Psychological Bulletin 140(6):1432-1463.
DOI: 10.1037/a0037559.
PMID: 25150680.

Use: retrieval/testing generally improves later retention relative to restudy; effortful recall is an important moderator.
Decision impact: active retrieval is a default candidate when durable recall is the endpoint, but not a universal substitute for understanding/application.

## S2 — Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017)
"Rethinking the Use of Tests: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Testing."
Review of Educational Research 87(3):659-701.
DOI: 10.3102/0034654316689306.

Use: practice testing benefits learning across many conditions, with meaningful moderators.
Decision impact: do not reduce retrieval practice to one fixed card/test format.

## S3 — Yang et al. (2021)
"Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review."
Psychological Bulletin.
PMID: 33683913.

Use: large classroom evidence base found a medium overall benefit of testing/quizzing; effects varied with feedback, repetition, format and timing.
Decision impact: retrieval should normally include corrective feedback and be adapted to the target/context.

## S4 — Cepeda et al. (2006)
"Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis."
Psychological Bulletin 132(3):354-380.
DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354.
PMID: 16719566.

Use: distributed practice generally improves retention; effective spacing depends jointly on gap and final retention interval.
Decision impact: reject universal interval schedules.

## S5 — Mawson & Kang (2025)
"The Distributed Practice Effect on Classroom Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review of Applied Research."
Behavioral Sciences 15(6):771.
DOI: 10.3390/bs15060771.
PMID: 40564553.

Use: applied classroom meta-analysis found a moderate benefit of distributed over massed practice.
Decision impact: spacing is operationally relevant beyond laboratory word-list tasks, while exact schedule remains context dependent.

## S6 — Rawson & Dunlosky (2013)
"Relearning attenuates the benefits and costs of spacing."
Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 142(4):1113-1129.
DOI: 10.1037/a0030498.
PMID: 23088488.

Use: when later relearning occurs, excessive initial overlearning may be inefficient.
Decision impact: prefer spaced successive relearning over grinding one item to excessive criterion in the first session.

## S7 — Rawson et al. (2018)
"Investigating and explaining the effects of successive relearning on long-term retention."
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied 24(1):57-71.
DOI: 10.1037/xap0000146.
PMID: 29431462.

Use: successive relearning can support long-term retention.
Decision impact: recurring retrieval + correction can be a study protocol for durable factual/definition targets.

## S8 — Bisra et al. (2018)
"Inducing Self-Explanation: a Meta-Analysis."
Educational Psychology Review 30:703-725.
DOI: 10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x.

Use: self-explanation prompts showed a positive overall effect across varied instructional conditions.
Decision impact: use self-explanation for conceptual relations when the target is understanding, not as a compulsory technique for every target.

## S9 — McCurdy et al. (2020)
"Theories of the generation effect and the impact of generation constraint: A meta-analytic review."
Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 27(6):1139-1165.
DOI: 10.3758/s13423-020-01762-3.
PMID: 32671573.

Use: self-generation can improve memory, with meaningful boundary conditions.
Decision impact: preserve learner generation but constrain it when source fidelity matters.

## S10 — Brunmair & Richter (2019)
"Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators."
Psychological Bulletin 145(11):1029-1052.
DOI: 10.1037/bul0000209.
PMID: 31556629.

Use: interleaving showed an overall positive effect but varied by material; some word-learning contexts favored blocking.
Decision impact: use interleaving selectively for discrimination/induction, not universally.

## S11 — Pan & Rickard (2018)
"Transfer of test-enhanced learning: Meta-analytic review and synthesis."
Psychological Bulletin.
PMID: 29733621.

Use: retrieval practice can support transfer, with meaningful moderators and weaker effects in some conditions.
Decision impact: when application/transfer is the endpoint, include transfer tasks rather than assuming factual recall automatically generalizes.

## S12 — Gereau & Mulligan (2026)
"Research on 'the testing effect' routinely conflates direct and forward testing effects: A meta-analysis of testing effects with free recall."
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition.
DOI: 10.1037/xlm0001634.
PMID: 42258276.

Use: current evidence distinguishes multiple testing effects and cautions against collapsing them into one mechanism.
Decision impact: phrase retrieval-practice claims narrowly and avoid claiming one mechanism explains every benefit.

## Conflict / boundary notes

- Retrieval practice strongly supports retention, but not every higher-order inference or transfer outcome automatically improves.
- Spacing is robust, but one fixed calendar is not evidence-based for all horizons/material.
- Interleaving is conditional.
- Self-explanation is useful for conceptual understanding but can redirect attention; it is not a universal memory drill.
- Generation benefits do not justify generating authoritative wording from memory.

## Freshness

Core evidence: stable/slow-changing.
Review trigger: major new meta-analysis that materially changes practical recommendations or exposes a boundary relevant to the skill.
