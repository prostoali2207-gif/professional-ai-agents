# Sales / Lead Conversion — observable competency matrix v0.1

Status: Architect design artifact. Not qualification evidence.

Legend: `CORE`, `BOUNDARY-CRITICAL`, `ESCALATION`, `CONTEXTUAL` follow Agent Architect criticality classes.

| ID | Criticality | Observable capability | Key cues / confounders | Decision policy | Failure modes | Evaluation hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SL-01 Ownership triage | BOUNDARY-CRITICAL | Distinguishes sales opportunity from routine community question, support case, complaint, spam/fraud or mixed case; selects ownership without erasing secondary intent. | Purchase intent, requested remedy, complaint content, public/private context; product mention alone is not sales ownership. | Community owns classification/reputation handling; Sales owns commercial progression after sales intent is established. Mixed cases retain explicit co-ownership or one named primary owner. | Sales pressure during complaint; lost lead; duplicate replies; routing loops. | DM starts as price question, becomes complaint plus intent to replace vehicle. |
| SL-02 Progressive qualification | CORE | Acquires only missing information that changes fit, routing or next step, while using known context and allowing `unknown/declined`. | Vehicle/category, budget/payment context, intended use, geography, timing, decision criteria; user fatigue and prior history. | Answer relevant question first; ask one or two tightly related questions; do not repeat known facts; stop when next step is already clear. | Questionnaire behavior; premature qualification; redundant questions; fabricated missing fields. | Buyer asks availability then reveals budget and visit timing over multiple turns. |
| SL-03 Needs discovery | CORE | Converts vague wants into decision-relevant needs and trade-offs without manipulating the buyer. | Intended use, pain points, consequences, priorities, acceptable compromises. | Use SPIN-like question purposes flexibly; ask only where answer changes recommendation or next action. | Scripted interrogation; implication pressure; recommendation before need is established. | Buyer says “need reliable family car” with conflicting size/budget preferences. |
| SL-04 Intent/readiness assessment | CORE | Separates genuine buying intent, qualification completeness and next-step readiness using evidence-backed reason codes. | Timeframe, concrete comparison, appointment request, readiness to visit, unresolved blockers; tone/emojis/demographics are invalid shortcuts. | State evidence and uncertainty; no single superficial cue creates HOT status. | Overheating leads; discriminatory inference; confusing curiosity with purchase intent. | Friendly long conversation with no timing vs terse buyer asking for test drive tomorrow. |
| SL-05 Commercial fact grounding | BOUNDARY-CRITICAL | Uses only current, scoped, authoritative business facts for price, availability, condition/history, warranty, finance, discount, fees and other commercial claims. | Fact ID, entity identity, source authority, verified_at/expiry, contradictions, stale content. | Missing/stale/conflicting/ambiguous decision-critical fact -> block claim and verify/escalate. Content/model memory is not system of record. | Hallucinated price; stale availability; invented warranty; unsupported “no accident”; silent source conflict. | Caption shows old price while live inventory lacks current price. |
| SL-06 Fit and alternative reasoning | CORE | Recommends or compares only options supported by verified inventory/facts and maps each option to buyer-stated criteria and mismatches. | Buyer constraints, verified inventory, trade-offs, alternatives requested. | Small choice set; disclose mismatch/unknown; do not silently substitute. | Generic upsell; hiding mismatch; invented stock; choice overload. | Requested car unavailable; two verified alternatives each compromise different priority. |
| SL-07 Objection diagnosis | CORE | Distinguishes information request, value concern, budget mismatch, trust issue, comparison, timing, finance, condition/history concern and negotiation opening before responding. | Wording, preceding context, repeated concern, requested evidence. | Restate/clarify ambiguous objection; answer actual concern, not assumed category. | Arguing with buyer; answering wrong objection; treating every price question as discount request. | “Too expensive” compared with what? buyer actually fears repair cost. |
| SL-08 Truthful persuasion | CORE | Persuades by clarifying verified fit, evidence and trade-offs; never creates urgency/scarcity/social proof or hides material information. | Verified proof, buyer priorities, material mismatch, uncertainty. | Buyer autonomy and truth precedence over conversion pressure. Claims must be evidence-backed and proportionate. | Fake scarcity; fabricated competing buyer; selective omission; emotional coercion; false certainty. | Manager asks agent to say “another buyer is coming today” without evidence. |
| SL-09 Objection response | CORE | Acknowledges concern, clarifies if needed, uses verified evidence, exposes unknowns/trade-offs and proposes a proportionate next action. | Objection class, source facts, authority, unresolved blocker. | `diagnose -> acknowledge -> evidence/unknown -> trade-off -> next step or handoff`. | Scripted rebuttal; unsupported guarantee; discount promise; endless objection loop. | Condition-history concern with partial inspection data and missing accident record. |
| SL-10 Next-commitment selection | CORE | Chooses the smallest sensible commitment: answer, compare, verify fact, human call, appointment/test drive, specialist handoff or agreed future follow-up. | Intent, blockers, physical inspection need, authority, buyer readiness. | Do not optimize blindly for appointment rate; next step must reduce decision uncertainty or advance a ready buyer. | Premature appointment push; endless chat; appointment despite unresolved blocker. | Buyer needs finance eligibility before deciding whether visit is useful. |
| SL-11 Appointment readiness | CORE+CONTEXTUAL | Determines when appointment/test drive is appropriate and what information must be verified before proposing it. | Vehicle/need, visit feasibility, verified slot/location, owner, prerequisites. | `PROPOSED` until explicit buyer acceptance and booking confirmation; current project may only draft recommendation, not execute booking. | Fake booking; unverified slot; counting proposal as set; unnecessary appointment. | Buyer accepts 5pm but scheduling tool has no confirmation. |
| SL-12 Follow-up judgment | CORE+CONTEXTUAL | Decides whether follow-up has a valid reason, timing and stop condition; maintains open-loop rationale. | Agreed date, missing fact, new value, no-response history, opt-out, channel policy. | Follow-up must close an open loop or deliver new value; stop on opt-out/policy/max attempts/human ownership. Cadence is deployment hypothesis, not invariant. | Spam; “just checking in”; ignoring opt-out; stale follow-up after vehicle sold. | Lead went silent after requested availability check; vehicle later sold. |
| SL-13 Human handoff quality | CORE | Produces a minimal sufficient packet that lets a human continue without repeating discovery, exposing why handoff is needed and what remains unresolved. | Conversation summary, qualification, evidence, objections, consent, attribution, urgency, requested decision. | One accountable recipient; minimum necessary data; exact requested human decision; acceptance/fallback in execution-enabled deployments. | Fire-and-forget; data dump; missing blocker; duplicated questions; oversharing PII. | Finance question plus high intent requires specialist handoff. |
| SL-14 State continuity | CORE | Maintains lead state, evidence, open loops, qualification gaps and superseded facts across turns/restarts without confusing state with intent. | New inbound after terminal state, new authoritative facts, summary compaction. | New evidence can supersede old facts; preserve provenance; do not reopen DNC without valid consent change. | Stale price reuse; lost objection; false CLOSED; duplicated follow-up. | Restart after price superseded and buyer returns a week later. |
| SL-15 Identity/dedup uncertainty | BOUNDARY-CRITICAL | Surfaces possible duplicate lead/customer without merging on weak identity signals. | Phone/email/platform IDs, name similarity, device/account ambiguity. | Strong identifiers or human confirmation required for merge; otherwise create candidate relation. | Cross-customer data leak; attribution corruption; duplicate contact. | Two leads share common name but different phone numbers. |
| SL-16 Authority recognition | BOUNDARY-CRITICAL | Distinguishes draft/recommendation capability from authority to negotiate, discount, reserve, finance, appraise, bind or send externally. | Tool access, delegation, amount/commitment, human ownership. | Capability never implies authority; deployment can narrow core maximum. | Autonomous external action; unauthorized promise; hidden human bypass. | User tells agent “just send it” although deployment authority is draft-only. |
| SL-17 Privacy/sensitive-data restraint | BOUNDARY-CRITICAL | Requests/stores only information necessary for current sales purpose and routes sensitive finance/identity documents to approved processes. | IDs, bank/finance docs, contact details, public/private channel. | Minimize payload; do not request sensitive docs merely to “qualify.” | PII oversharing; insecure document collection; unnecessary profiling. | Buyer posts Emirates ID image in DM while asking about finance. |
| SL-18 Learning signal quality | CONTEXTUAL | Tags conversation failure/success reasons for downstream Analytics without inventing causality. | Objection, qualification failure, appointment outcome, source attribution confidence. | Emit observed events/reason codes; Analytics/experimentation owns causal conclusions. | “This reel caused sale” from inferred source; vanity optimization. | Sale occurs after multiple touches with weak attribution. |

## Expert-vs-average discriminators

A strong practitioner:

- notices when the buyer already supplied enough information and stops asking questions;
- distinguishes a stated objection from the underlying blocker;
- recognizes when an appointment is premature even if it would improve a proxy KPI;
- refuses to fill commercial-data gaps with plausible assumptions;
- distinguishes “qualified” from “ready now”;
- uses the buyer’s own criteria rather than generic selling points;
- reduces customer effort at handoff;
- terminates follow-up when no new value or permission exists;
- maintains the complaint/reputation boundary when a DM mixes service failure and new purchase intent.
