# Brand Naming Practitioner — profession model and architecture v0.1

Status: Architect research artifact. Not a SKILL. Not qualified.
Issue: #275
Date: 2026-09-04

## 1. Decision summary

The target is a reusable **Brand Naming Practitioner capability**, not a generic branding agent and not a visual-identity role.

Agent Architect action: **BUILD NEW reusable capability package**.

Do not create a new top-level professional core merely because naming is distinct work. The least-complex architecture is:

`approved strategy/identity constraints -> Brand Naming capability -> deterministic/live screening -> specialist handoffs -> decision-ready shortlist`

The capability is portable across company, product/service, project and personal-brand naming when the same professional invariants apply. Domain, language, jurisdiction, platform and organization context remain external bindings.

## 2. Why the capability exists

Applied work has now exposed naming in materially different contexts:
- an automotive-parts brand (`Spline`) where naming risk, category distinctiveness, domain/handle availability and confusion with existing brands matter;
- a personal professional brand where an Instagram/social handle must be memorable, durable, truthful, discoverable and not imply unsupported expertise.

Existing internal cores do not own the professional judgment required to turn an approved positioning into a defensible name.

The work is also recognized as a standalone professional service by naming firms such as Lexicon, Catchword and NameStormers, with repeated patterns: discovery/brief -> naming parameters/territories -> broad ideation -> evaluation/screening -> shortlist/presentation -> legal/linguistic validation.

## 3. Professional responsibility

### Owns

1. **Naming brief translation**
   - convert approved positioning, audience, category, tone and constraints into naming objectives and decision criteria;
   - expose missing inputs that would materially change naming territory.

2. **Naming territory design**
   - create conceptually distinct semantic/metaphoric/linguistic territories;
   - preserve open creative space before convergence.

3. **Candidate generation**
   - generate a sufficiently broad pool across materially different constructions rather than paraphrasing one idea;
   - use descriptive, suggestive/evocative, coined, compound, founder/personal-name and other forms when justified by the brief.

4. **Linguistic craft**
   - reason about meaning, morphology, phonetics, sound symbolism, spelling, pronunciation, visual word shape and transcribability;
   - treat these as context-dependent cues, not universal magic formulas.

5. **Professional evaluation and shortlist judgment**
   - compare candidates on brand fit, distinctiveness, memorability/processing, accessibility, longevity/scalability, linguistic/cultural risk, protectability/screening status and practical channel fit;
   - preserve trade-offs rather than collapse all judgment into one arbitrary score.

6. **Preliminary screening**
   - current competitor/common-law web search;
   - domain/social/platform availability checks when required;
   - obvious collision/similarity screening;
   - linguistic/cultural screening at the level supported by available evidence.

7. **Decision-ready presentation**
   - show finalists in realistic verbal/digital contexts;
   - explain rationale, risks, unresolved checks and handoffs;
   - label facts vs hypotheses vs unverified status.

8. **Personal-brand handle mode**
   - apply the same naming principles to durable social identities;
   - avoid unsupported titles/credentials;
   - prefer portability and findability when the brief requires them.

### Does not own

- upstream brand/business positioning, audience selection, offer or commercial strategy;
- visual identity, logo or typography system;
- formal legal trademark clearance or legal opinion;
- certified linguistic/cultural clearance in languages/markets where specialist review is required;
- registration/purchase of domains, trademarks or handles;
- publishing/account mutations;
- final stakeholder approval.

## 4. Core work products

A strong practitioner produces:

1. **Naming Brief**
   - named entity;
   - purpose/category;
   - approved positioning and audience;
   - desired perceptions;
   - naming scope/type;
   - constraints and prohibited implications;
   - markets/languages;
   - required platforms/assets;
   - decision owner and risk level.

2. **Naming Criteria Contract**
   - project-specific criteria and non-negotiable hard fails fixed before candidate selection;
   - distinction between universal professional checks and project-specific preferences.

3. **Naming Territory Map**
   - 3+ materially distinct concept/language territories when open exploration is warranted;
   - rationale and what each territory should/should not communicate.

4. **Candidate Pool**
   - broad exploration; internal working pool may be large;
   - no claim that volume alone proves quality.

5. **Screening Ledger**
   - candidate -> current web/category evidence -> domain/handle status -> linguistic flags -> obvious confusion/trademark-risk flags -> verification timestamp/source;
   - `FREE/TAKEN` never asserted from memory.

6. **Shortlist Decision**
   - 3–5 serious finalists when enough survive;
   - criteria-level trade-offs;
   - risk/uncertainty;
   - required specialist checks;
   - recommendation only when evidence supports convergence.

7. **Handoff Packet**
   - chosen or surviving finalists;
   - exact spelling/case;
   - pronunciation guidance when needed;
   - verified availability state and timestamp;
   - legal/localization unknowns;
   - visual-identity handoff constraints.

## 5. Tacit professional judgment

### J1 — Fit without over-description

A name can be relevant without literally describing the offering. Over-descriptive names often reduce distinctiveness and scalability; overly abstract names can impose explanation cost. The correct balance depends on category maturity, brand strategy, acquisition context and protectability.

### J2 — Distinctive does not mean random

Unusual spelling or invented syllables are not automatically distinctive in a useful sense. The name must remain cognitively processable and strategically interpretable enough for the intended audience.

### J3 — Memorability is not a vibe

Memorability is affected by processing, sound, spelling/transcription, existing semantic hooks and repeated context. A candidate that "looks clever" but cannot be repeated or spelled after hearing is a meaningful risk.

### J4 — Sound symbolism is bounded evidence

Empirical research shows that phonetic structure can influence perceived attributes and preference, including cross-language effects. It is a cue, not a deterministic naming law. The practitioner must not claim that one vowel or consonant universally creates a desired perception.

### J5 — Availability is multi-layered

An exact domain being taken does not automatically kill a strong name, and an available domain does not establish trademark safety. Social handles, common-law use, category collisions and trademark similarity are distinct checks.

### J6 — Similarity is broader than exact match

Trademark/confusion risk can arise from similar sound, appearance, meaning or commercial impression for related goods/services. Preliminary screening must therefore go beyond exact-string search, while formal legal clearance remains outside authority.

### J7 — Linguistic screening is not machine translation

A literal translation check misses slang, phonetic overlap, cultural/historical associations and pronunciation/spelling problems. High-stakes cross-market decisions require native/in-country or qualified language review.

### J8 — User preference is not evidence

A stakeholder favorite must not bypass the same screen as other candidates. Naming is subjective, but professional selection still uses explicit criteria, evidence and trade-off reasoning.

### J9 — Broad generation before hard convergence

Premature evaluation narrows exploration and creates anchoring. Generate across materially different territories before selecting, except when the task is explicitly a single-name audit or a constrained extension of an existing naming system.

### J10 — A name does not need to tell the whole brand story

A name is one component of brand identity. Requiring it to encode every feature, benefit and positioning claim usually produces literal or overloaded names.

### J11 — Personal-brand handles carry truth constraints

A handle such as `ali_ai_expert` implies a role/claim. If the claim ledger cannot support that identity, naming must not manufacture it for perceived authority.

### J12 — Tool failure is an evidence state

If an availability or registry check cannot execute, the result is `UNVERIFIED`, not "probably free." Lack of evidence cannot be converted into availability.

## 6. Stable vs live/context knowledge

### Stable professional invariants
- strategy precedes naming;
- criteria before convergence;
- divergence across real territories;
- distinguish fit, distinctiveness, accessibility, longevity and protectability;
- evaluate spoken + written behavior;
- similarity is not exact-string-only;
- web/domain/social screening is not legal clearance;
- uncertainty must remain visible;
- legal/localization/visual handoffs preserve boundaries.

### Live context
- current competitors and names in use;
- domain/handle availability;
- current platform username rules;
- current trademark databases and legal status;
- current cultural/news associations;
- organization-specific positioning and constraints.

### Domain specialization
Possible but not assumed:
- pharmaceuticals/regulated nomenclature;
- technical package/ecosystem names;
- geographic/cultural markets;
- personal-brand naming;
- naming architectures with many sub-brands.

A specialization does not transfer its qualification automatically.

## 7. Professional evidence anchors

### Professional practice

Lexicon's current naming process emphasizes:
- a strategic understanding of brand essence and target audience;
- memorability/processing;
- trademarkability;
- linguistic/cultural implications;
- research as a decision tool;
- semantics, structure and sound.

Catchword describes naming as a structured process spanning brand naming strategy, creative directions, broad generation, selection and trademark/domain/social availability screening. It explicitly warns against choosing by immediate preference alone.

NameStormers separates discovery, ideation, legal/market screening and presentation, and treats linguistic screening as native-market work that includes slang/phonetic/cultural checks.

### Official legal boundary

USPTO guidance requires a clearance search and explains that confusing similarity can arise from sound, appearance, meaning or overall commercial impression, in combination with related goods/services. A web search or exact match check is therefore not formal clearance.

WIPO materials support distinctiveness as a core trademark concept and note that personal names may serve as marks when they distinguish source, while legal treatment remains jurisdiction-specific.

### Empirical linguistic evidence

Research in consumer psychology and marketing supports:
- sound symbolism can influence brand perception;
- some sound-symbolism effects generalize across languages;
- spelling/transcription characteristics can affect later recall.

These findings support linguistic competence but do not justify deterministic "sound recipes."

## 8. Architecture alternatives

### A — Reuse Market & Competitive Intelligence as naming owner
REJECT.
It can supply current category/competitor evidence but its qualified boundary stops at evidence and bounded implications.

### B — Reuse Growth Strategy as naming owner
REJECT as sole owner.
Strategy can supply positioning/objective constraints. Its frozen candidate does not encode naming craft, linguistic judgment or availability/legal screening.

### C — Reuse Social Content Creative
REJECT.
It assumes supplied brand/language context and owns creative execution, not brand-name creation.

### D — Reuse Visual Design / Brand Identity
REJECT as naming owner.
Visual identity consumes a selected name. The Spline project skill explicitly treats naming as already decided.

### E — Import an external naming SKILL directly
REJECT for REUSE/ADAPT.
External candidate sources show plausible procedures but lack local construct validation, provenance/evidence integration and independent qualification under this repository.

### F — Build a new top-level professional core
REJECT for now.
The responsibility is coherent but narrow enough to package as a modular reusable capability. A separate core creates lifecycle/coordination cost without demonstrated need.

### G — BUILD NEW reusable Brand Naming capability
SELECTED.
This preserves a real professional boundary without inflating the core catalog.

## 9. Expert-vs-average discriminators

An average generator:
- asks for a vibe and emits 10 names;
- overuses trendy suffixes and vague tech words;
- treats an available domain as proof;
- gives one composite score and hides trade-offs;
- follows user favorite;
- guesses handle availability;
- calls a quick web search legal clearance.

A strong practitioner:
- locks the naming brief and decision criteria;
- explores materially different territories;
- tests spoken/written/transcription behavior;
- separates semantic, phonetic and cultural signals;
- screens current collisions and marks uncertainty;
- preserves multiple serious options until screening is mature;
- knows when exact .com/handle parity is optional vs hard;
- escalates legal and linguistic certification correctly;
- presents a name as a strategic asset with explicit trade-offs, not as a clever word.

## 10. Revisit triggers

Reconsider top-level-core status only if:
- naming work expands into stable naming architecture/governance across large brand portfolios;
- recurring professional work requires persistent multi-session stakeholder/process ownership beyond a modular capability;
- repeated compositions show coordination failure from capability packaging;
- evaluation demonstrates a distinct authority/runtime boundary that cannot be safely attached to existing brand/strategy systems.
