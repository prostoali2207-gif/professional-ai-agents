## Interpretation rules

### Naturalness is not blandness
E20/E22 do not justify rejecting edgy, coined, poetic or unusual names. They justify testing whether the unusualness feels coherent and speakable rather than conspicuously engineered.

### Authenticity is not user preference
“Authentic to the brand” requires an approved identity/brief. A user saying “this feels like me” is useful stakeholder evidence but not standalone proof of market or naming quality.

### Collision relatedness
USPTO E23 is legal guidance and cannot be mechanically imported into all social-handle decisions. The transferable principle is narrower: **similarity without contextual relatedness is not the same as realistic source/identity confusion**. Formal trademark conclusions remain legal handoff.

### Production evidence
E26 identifies repeated failure mechanisms. Exact chat examples must not be copied into future hidden qualification fixtures as gold answers. Development regressions may use transformed/analogous cases.
