# Direction selection — serious short-form business reel

## Decision

**Selected: Direction B** (diagram-led, restrained spatial system), with **Direction A retained as a live alternative**, not discarded. **Direction C is rejected** for this brief.

This is a selection at `DEVELOPMENT_DIRECTION` status. It is not a craft pass and not an approval to animate full length — B still has to survive the styleframe/prototype gate before any full motion authoring.

## Assumptions stated

- The message is approved and unchanged; all three directions were judged against the same brief, so communication correctness is not the discriminator.
- "Serious business reel" means a mobile-first, sound-off-likely, credibility-carrying piece where the viewer must retain one idea, not admire the graphics.
- The structural edit is locked, or will be locked, before B's timing is authored (MG3-03). If structure is still moving, I animate nothing beyond bounded exploratory frames.
- There is at least one relational or state-change beat in the message. If diagnosis shows the message is a single assertion with no mechanism to explain, the selection flips to A — see the tie-break below.

## Comparative reasoning (MG3-02)

I compared the three on the same brief, scored separately rather than as one taste scalar:

**Communication effectiveness.** B is strongest: a spatial system can carry relationship, transfer or state change, so the graphic does work the narration does not repeat (MG3-06/MG3-08). A communicates by assertion — excellent for a claim, thin for a mechanism. C communicates no better than B while costing far more attention.

**Visual-system coherence.** A and B both read as one designed system. C's layered UI cards and effects are the classic `GENERIC_VISUAL_SYSTEM` failure mode: it reads as dashboard/app components and template language, which in a business context actively undercuts the seriousness the brief is buying. Its decoration is not brief-driven.

**Composition, proportion, typography.** A's negative space is its argument and it is the cheapest to get right. B needs a real grid and disciplined type roles or it degrades into labels-and-boxes; that is its main risk and where I will spend review effort. C's density suppresses hierarchy — the viewer does not know where to look first, and per MG3-03 the repair for that is removing layers, not adding emphasis, i.e. rebuilding C into B or A.

**Motion information gain (MG3-09).** B's motion has definable jobs: represented change, causal/temporal explanation, attention direction, each with a hold time and a removal criterion. A's motion is deliberately minimal — legitimate restraint, not under-design. C's motion is largely self-justifying ("dynamic"), which is not an eligible job. Combined attention load in C fails.

**Authored distinctiveness.** B highest, A close behind through restraint, C lowest despite having the most surface polish. Polish is not distinctiveness.

**Mobile final impression.** A and B survive phone size; C's layered cards and effect stack will not hold up at small size and low contrast conditions.

## Actions

1. Run motion-opportunity and state-change diagnosis on the beats; confirm B's mechanism preserves identity/causality without implying measured magnitude or timestamps that the approved evidence does not support (MG3-02 information integrity, MG3-08).
2. Build B styleframes — opening, peak explanatory, resolved — plus one short prototype demonstrating the actual motion language and curve quality (MG3-13). Build A's peak frame in parallel as the fallback; it is cheap insurance.
3. Confirm the runtime/asset-authoring route can actually author B's spatial system and custom graphic language (MG3-14). If it cannot, I choose A deliberately and say so — I will not silently collapse B into CSS cards, which is how directions become C.
4. Open the overlay ledger for B's labels, mapping each to an approved beat, time range and owner (MG3-04).
5. Reserve one element of C: if a specific effect there serves a real job (a single depth or light cue), it migrates into B as a controlled device, not as a style.

## Tie-break rule recorded

If styleframe review shows B's diagram is carrying labels rather than a mechanism, switch to A rather than repairing B with more decoration.
