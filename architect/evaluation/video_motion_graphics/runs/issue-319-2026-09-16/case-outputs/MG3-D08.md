## Diagnosis

This is not a font problem, and "more professional" is not a diagnosis I can act on. What you have is a **typography and hierarchy failure inside a technically valid render**.

Readable text inside safe zones satisfies the parent's caption/title technical checks (VE-07) and my technical QC only. It says nothing about craft. Recorded state: `TECHNICAL_PASS` / `CRAFT_FAIL`.

Defect classification: `TYPOGRAPHY` (`GENERIC_TEMPLATE_TYPOGRAPHY`) as primary, `HIERARCHY` as coupled, with a secondary `GENERIC_VISUAL_SYSTEM` flag on the pills.

Three observable causes, none of which a new typeface fixes:

1. **No role differentiation.** Title, label and caption carry the same visual weight, so the frame has no first-look target. The viewer scans all three layers in parallel and lands on whichever is closest to centre. Hierarchy is currently being decided by position accident, not by design.
2. **Line breaks set by container width, not by meaning.** Text is wrapping where the box ends rather than at phrase boundaries. At phone size that forces a re-read on almost every title, which reads as amateur even when every glyph is legible.
3. **Pills are default containers, not authored form.** They are doing one real job (contrast over footage) and three unintended ones: they equalise all text by giving everything the same frame, they add graphic language that belongs to no motif in the piece, and they read as presentation-UI/template.

## What I am not doing

No fashionable font swap. No repairing generic type with colour, italic, outline, shadow or a glow. No simply enlarging the title — that trades one flat hierarchy for a louder flat hierarchy.

## Actions

1. **Type role map, three roles maximum:** title/statement, label/naming, caption/speech. Each role gets distinct decisions on at least two axes (scale plus one of weight, case, tracking, position), so difference survives phone-size viewing. One-axis differentiation is what produced the current flatness.
2. **Scale relationships chosen by observation**, not a nominal ratio: I set candidates, export a still, and judge at representative phone size.
3. **Authored line breaks.** Manual breaks at phrase boundaries, titles capped at two lines with a character-per-line ceiling, auto-wrap disabled so breaks cannot re-flow on re-render. Wording unchanged — that stays the copy owner's.
4. **Pill audit.** Removed by default; each one must re-earn itself. Where a contrast ground is genuinely needed over footage, I replace the pill with a device that belongs to the piece's own graphic language — an anchored field, a baseline rule, a mask reveal, or plain negative space — and check it for scene-to-scene coherence (MG3-01) rather than per-overlay cleanliness.
5. **Emphasis logic.** Any caption accent must carry a semantic or attention function (MG3-07). Repeated accent that makes everything loud is the same failure in a different costume.
6. **Restraint before emphasis.** Where all layers still compete, I delay or remove a layer rather than adding weight (MG-03 repair rule).
7. **Re-gate before re-animating.** Three styleframes — opening, peak explanatory, resolved — reviewed statically; a short motion prototype only if timing or entry/exit logic changes. No full-length re-render before that passes (MG3-13).
