## Comparative reasoning (MG3-02, same brief, axes judged separately)

- **Communication effectiveness:** A is legible and predictable; card stacks and stock transitions carry a low comprehension risk but also a low information gain. B's single original explanatory motif is the only place either piece actually explains rather than lists. Advantage B, conditional on the motif being tested for state-change fit (MG3-08) rather than assumed.
- **Visual-system coherence:** A is a sequence of individually clean overlays, not one designed system — that is exactly the generic-system failure, and polish is what is hiding it. B holds scene-to-scene coherence. Clear advantage B.
- **Composition / proportion / spacing:** B's spacing is purposeful; A's is template-derived. Advantage B.
- **Typography craft:** A's kinetic type is predictable and reads as default treatment (`GENERIC_TEMPLATE_TYPOGRAPHY`). B has custom typographic rhythm. Advantage B.
- **Graphic language:** A borrows presentation-UI/card vocabulary with no brief-driven reason. B authors its own. Advantage B.
- **Motion timing and information gain (MG3-09):** A's transitions are available, not earned — no represented change, no removal criterion. B is quieter, which is only a virtue where the quiet is authored.
- **Integration and depth:** B's subject integration is better; A treats the subject as a backplate under cards. Advantage B.
- **Authored distinctiveness vs restraint risk:** A is surface polish mistaken for craft. B's real exposure is the opposite error — under-design read as restraint. That is the one thing I verify before committing.

"Polished" is not a craft finding, and "quieter" is not a defect. A's finish is cheap to reproduce; B's system is the expensive part and the part worth keeping.
