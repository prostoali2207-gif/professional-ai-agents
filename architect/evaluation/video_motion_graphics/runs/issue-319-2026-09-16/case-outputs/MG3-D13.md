## Decision

The comparison stays static. Three of the four proposed elements come out; one bounded motion beat goes in, and it is not any of the three.

**Removed:** bouncing icons, floating labels, animated border.
**Kept/authored:** a single staggered reveal of the two compared sides, plus one emphasis beat on the decisive difference at the moment narration asserts it. Nothing loops.

## Reasoning

"Should feel more dynamic" is an aesthetic preference, not a communication job. The comparison already clears the bar that matters: it is understood immediately at phone size. Motion added on top of an already-solved comprehension problem can only spend attention, and attention load is judged combined, not per effect.

Running each proposal through the motion-information-gain test (`job -> represented change -> viewer inference -> timing/hold -> removal criterion`):

- **Bouncing icons** — no represented change. The icons denote fixed categories; bouncing implies they are active, alive or changing when they are not. It also pulls the eye away from the one thing the viewer must read, the difference between the two sides. Fails; no removal criterion can be stated because it never ends.
- **Floating labels** — actively harmful. Labels are the anchoring layer for a comparison; continuous drift breaks the fixed spatial relationship the comparison depends on, degrades readability of small mobile type, and risks collision with the compared elements. Composition and hierarchy repair is "remove or delay a layer," not "make a layer move."
- **Animated border** — decorative frame motion with no referent in the content. This is the class of element that makes a piece read as generic template rather than authored, and it competes with the frame edge on a vertical phone crop where margins are already tight.
- **Staggered reveal (kept)** — job: sequence the read so the viewer takes in side A, then side B, then the delta, rather than scanning a finished block. Represented change: information arriving in the intended order. Inference: these two things are being set against each other. Timing: short, eased offsets, then a full hold on the static composition for the rest of the beat. Removal criterion: if the hold is too short to read both sides at phone size, cut the stagger and cut to the finished static frame.
- **Delta emphasis (kept)** — job: land the point at the moment narration claims it. One scale/weight or value shift on the decisive side, settling and staying settled. Removal criterion: if narration does not assert a winner at that timecode, it goes.

Hard boundary: no motion here may imply magnitude, rate or trend. If the comparison is conceptual rather than measured, easing that stretches or grows an element toward a value would manufacture a quantitative claim, so growth-style animation is out regardless of taste.
