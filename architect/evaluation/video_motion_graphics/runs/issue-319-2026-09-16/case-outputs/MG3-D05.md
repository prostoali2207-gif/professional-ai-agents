# MG3-D05 — Workflow decision: animating diagrams against an unlocked rough edit

## Decision

No. We do not fully animate the three diagrams now. The structural edit is materially unstable — two sentences may be cut and the middle-section order is undecided — so full authoring is blocked under MG3-03 (structural edit lock before motion). What we do instead is run bounded, deliberately cheap exploration that reduces uncertainty without creating sunk cost: styleframes plus one short motion-language prototype, and no full-length authoring until the cut is locked.

Status recorded: `DEVELOPMENT_DIRECTION`, structural edit `UNLOCKED`. Craft state `CRAFT_NOT_OBSERVED`.

## Why this is not conservatism

The team's assumption is that animating early saves time. On diagram-heavy explanatory work it usually costs time, because the two open questions attack exactly the layers animation depends on.

Two sentences possibly removed changes narration duration, which changes entry/exit timing, hold length for comprehension, and speech synchronization for every element in that beat (MG-05, MG3-09). Those are not offsets you slide; keyframe density and easing were authored against a specific phrase.

Undecided middle-section order is worse. Diagram order carries causality. If the middle reorders, a diagram authored as "old state migrates to new state" may now arrive before its own setup, and the same animation reads as a different claim. Under MG3-08 the correct representation — persistent object changing state, transfer/migration, accumulation, relationship change — is chosen from the sequence. Choose it from the wrong sequence and the mechanism itself is wrong, not just the timing. That is the re-do we are avoiding: not tweaks, but concept re-selection after full authoring, which then re-triggers the whole prototype/QC chain (MG3-03 invalidation rule).

Three complex diagrams multiplies this by three, and by MG3-01 they must read as one visual system, so a mid-stream concept change on one propagates to the other two.

## What I will do now

1. Semantic beat map of the current cut, marking the two candidate sentences and the middle-section block as volatile. Diagrams anchored to volatile beats get flagged.
2. Motion opportunity map: confirm each of the three diagrams still earns its place, and for each define `communication job -> represented change -> intended viewer inference -> timing/hold -> removal criterion` (MG3-09). This work is order-independent and survives the cut changing.
3. Divergence per diagram: at least three materially different mechanisms (MG-01), tested against MG3-08 representations, not three colorways.
4. Styleframes only — opening, peak explanatory, resolved state — for the selected direction on each diagram, judged as one system (MG3-13, MG3-01). Static hierarchy failures get repaired here, where repair is cheap.
5. One short motion-language prototype on the single most stable diagram, demonstrating actual curves, hold and entry/exit logic. One, not three. It calibrates the motion language the other two will inherit.
6. Runtime/asset-authoring route check (MG3-14) in parallel. If the selected direction needs vector/texture craft the available surface cannot author, I raise it now and either route it or deliberately pick a simpler direction — I will not quietly collapse it into generic cards.
7. Overlay ledger (MG3-04) opened but not closed: overlay text mapped to approved beat and owner. Time ranges stay provisional until lock.

## What I need from the team

A decision on the two sentences and the middle-section order, or an explicit instruction to lock the current cut and treat later changes as a paid revision. That is the gating item, and it is theirs, not mine — this is a `NEEDS_UPSTREAM_REVISION` dependency on the edit owner, not a craft blocker.

If they lock today, styleframes and the prototype are ready to integrate immediately and almost nothing is lost. If they animate first, expect to re-author timing on all three diagrams and to re-select at least one mechanism.
