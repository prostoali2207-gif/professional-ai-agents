Three sibling cards is a *set* mechanism. It carries the grammar of comparison or enumeration: separate bounding boxes, separate origins, equal weight, no shared anchor. The content is the opposite — one persistent object acquiring attributes. So the visual mechanism contradicts the approved meaning, and the viewer's first inference ("three customers") is the wrong one. **B: dossier accumulation.** Same persistent object, treated as a document gaining layers and a final approval seal. Stronger brand character; risk of skeuomorphic decoration and a heavier graphic language than the brief needs.

**C: record travelling a spine.** One object moves left-to-right through identity -> preference -> approval stations, collecting attributes. Best if the brief needs to emphasise process handoff, but the three stations can re-import the original three-things misreading.

Select A, borrowing B's identity-block treatment for the locked header. Hold C unless the brief later foregrounds process ownership.
