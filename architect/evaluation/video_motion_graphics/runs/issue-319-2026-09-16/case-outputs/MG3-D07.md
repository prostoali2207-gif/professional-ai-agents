## Decision

The reference set is built from the current strong short-form explainers with comparable information density. Those are the fit-for-purpose core. The award-winning title sequence and the luxury landing page enter the set only as bounded mechanism-diversity sources, each with an explicit non-transferable note. The mediocre reel templates enter as negative references — a named list of what we are avoiding — not as craft anchors. I am not treating fame or prestige as evidence of fit (MG3-05).

## Reasoning

Our brief is a mobile talking-head explainer. That fixes the real constraints before any reference is admitted: a live human subject holds primary hierarchy, graphics are secondary and captions tertiary (MG-03); the piece is watched at phone size, frequently muted, in a scrolling feed; and the information density is high enough that comprehension, hold time and caption legibility dominate the visual decisions.

The award-winning film title solves a completely different problem — a long-form, high-attention, sound-on, no-subject-competition, no-caption, cinema-scale opening whose job is tone and authorship, not explanation. Copying its surface language into a 9:16 explainer imports heavy full-frame typography that will fight the speaker's face and the caption band. Its transferable value is narrow but real: authored type as primary form, and motion as a deliberate rhythmic event rather than uniform movement.

The luxury landing page is a self-paced, hover-driven, mouse-scrolled, desktop-width, static-composition medium. Its proportion, negative-space discipline and restrained palette/value relationships are transferable to our composition system (MG3-01). Its scroll-triggered reveals, long slow eases and thin small type are not — a viewer cannot pause our frame, and thin small type fails phone-size legibility (MG3-06).

The strong current short-form explainers are the only references solving our actual problem: subject plus overlay plus caption at our density, on our surface. They are where I extract mechanism, hold time, entry/exit timing and layer-count discipline.

The mediocre templates are still useful evidence — they are the concrete definition of the GENERIC_VISUAL_SYSTEM failure mode we must not converge on, and naming them prevents "premium" from becoming the diagnosis.

## Concrete actions

1. Inspect rendered artifacts, not stills or thumbnails, at phone size with sound off and then on. A reference judged from a screenshot is not decision-useful.
2. For each admitted reference, write one row: `problem -> mechanism -> why it works -> transferable principle -> non-transferable surface expression -> derivative risk`.
3. Cap the set at roughly four to six explainers plus the two adjacent-category items. Stop admitting explainers once new ones only repeat mechanisms already captured — additional examples past that point have low plausible decision value.
4. Require at least two distinct mechanism families across the set so no single reference becomes the style anchor (MG-08). If the explainers all use the same lower-third-plus-card pattern, that is a signal to deliberately seek a different mechanism family — persistent-object state change, transfer/migration, relationship change (MG3-08) — rather than accept convergence.
5. Flag derivative risk explicitly where any reference has a recognizable signature combination. High-recognition surfaces are rejected for direct reuse absent explicit licence and professional justification.
6. Carry the set into DIVERGE as mechanism input only. Three materially different directions still get authored independently; styleframes are then judged against the brief under MG3-02, not against the references' surfaces.
