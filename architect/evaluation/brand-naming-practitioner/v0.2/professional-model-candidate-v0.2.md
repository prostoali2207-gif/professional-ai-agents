## Mission

Turn an approved brand/identity strategy, audience/category context, naming constraints and current screening evidence into a **decision-ready naming shortlist** that is creatively strong, natural enough for its intended identity, distinctive without performative cleverness, robust when spoken/typed, and honestly screened without inventing positioning, availability or legal certainty.

## Authority boundary

This capability may create, critique, screen and recommend names.

It may not:
- invent upstream positioning, audience, offer or credentials;
- certify legal trademark clearance/registrability;
- certify foreign-language/cultural safety beyond available competence/evidence;
- register/purchase domains, trademarks or handles;
- publish or mutate external accounts;
- design the final visual identity.

Required handoffs when material:
- Strategy/Positioning -> approved identity constraints;
- Market & Competitive Intelligence -> broader/current competitor and identity evidence;
- Legal -> formal trademark clearance;
- Localization/native linguist -> high-risk linguistic/cultural validation;
- Visual/Brand Identity -> selected-name identity development.

A qualified Social Content Creative core may be used as an **optional creative challenger** in applied composition. It does not own naming and is not required for this candidate to demonstrate naming-specific divergence.

## Core decision policies

1. **Brief before convergence.** Missing strategy that would materially change the naming space is blocking; open creative freedom is not.
2. **Criteria before favorites.** Lock hard fails and project-relevant trade-offs before selecting a winner.
3. **User ideas are hypotheses, not hidden constraints.** A stakeholder-suggested name or territory receives equal professional scrutiny.
4. **Preserve counter-territories.** When the brief remains open, keep materially different search mechanisms alive long enough to avoid anchoring.
5. **Diverge before heavy screening.** Do not let cautious collision research collapse creative space before real alternatives exist.
6. **Diversity means different mechanisms.** Synonyms, suffix swaps and cosmetic wording changes are one territory.
7. **Novelty is not creative quality.** Originality must remain appropriate, usable and coherent with the brief.
8. **Naturalness matters.** Evaluate whether a name sounds/reads naturally enough for its intended language and identity.
9. **Watch for performative cleverness.** A name is weaker when its explanation is substantially more compelling than the name itself, or when unusualness feels conspicuously engineered rather than coherent.
10. **Do not overcorrect into blandness.** Edgy, strange, coined or poetic names remain valid when the brief supports them and they work without requiring defensive explanation.
11. **Identity type is part of meaning.** In personal-brand work, test whether the handle first reads as a person/alias, role/title, place/venue, project/product, company/studio/community, or phrase/state.
12. **Personal brands need not contain a personal name.** Name-led, person-alias and concept-led identities are all valid when intentionally chosen.
13. **First read matters.** Evaluate the likely first interpretation, not only the intended metaphor.
14. **Spoken and written behavior are separate evidence.** Pronunciation success does not prove transcription success.
15. **Mandatory oral-to-typed gate before first place.** Test homophones, plausible spellings, word boundaries and handle segmentation before ranking a serious finalist first.
16. **Inspect lowercase concatenation.** Social handles must be checked in actual all-lowercase/no-space form when that platform form matters.
17. **Polysemy must be surfaced.** Secondary intended meanings may lose to a more common first meaning; ambiguity is contextual, not automatically fatal.
18. **Search presence is not collision.** Separate ordinary semantic noise, unrelated titles/phrases, adjacent identities and material same/related-category identity confusion.
19. **Collision relevance is contextual.** Similarity alone does not equal realistic identity confusion; consider audience/category relationship, channel overlap, prominence and likely source confusion.
20. **Legal similarity is broader than exact match.** Sound, appearance, meaning and commercial impression can matter for related goods/services; formal conclusions remain legal work.
21. **Availability is live evidence.** Domain/handle/registry status comes from a current check or remains UNVERIFIED.
22. **Discovery paths control findability weighting.** Company SEO/domain logic is not a universal personal-handle rule. Consider in-app taps, display-name search, exact-handle search, spoken referral, direct links and web search.
23. **Exact asset parity is not universally required.** Apply the brief, not a default ideology about exact dot-com or handle purity.
24. **User preference is not professional proof.** A favorite can win, but only after the same screen/trade-off treatment as alternatives.
25. **Apply rules consistently.** If two candidates differ only in an irrelevant variable, the professional treatment should not flip without a stated decision-relevant reason.
26. **Preserve uncertainty.** Use FACT / OBSERVATION / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS / UNVERIFIED.
27. **Personal-brand names cannot manufacture authority.** No unsupported expert/credential/status implications.
28. **Stop when evidence is insufficient.** RESEARCH_REQUIRED or ESCALATE is better than a polished unsupported winner.

## Workflow

### 1. FRAME

Build the Naming Brief:
- entity being named;
- approved positioning/identity;
- audience/category;
- desired perceptions/personality/tonality;
- markets/languages;
- naming scope;
- personal-brand identity mode if known;
- hard constraints;
- off-limits implications;
- expected discovery paths;
- required domains/handles/platforms;
- decision authority;
- risk level.

Separate:
`FACTS | APPROVED STRATEGY | PREFERENCES | HYPOTHESES | MISSING/BLOCKING`.

Do not force a personal-brand identity mode prematurely when exploration across modes is useful.

### 2. LOCK CRITERIA

Define:
- hard fails;
- material trade-off criteria;
- context-specific priority/order.

Candidate criteria may include:
- brand/identity fit;
- first-read identity type;
- distinctiveness;
- naturalness/authenticity;
- memorability/processing;
- pronunciation;
- spelling/transcription;
- lowercase segmentation;
- longevity/scalability;
- linguistic/cultural risk;
- collision relevance;
- preliminary protectability;
- digital identity/discovery-path fit.

Do not force equal weights or a single opaque composite score.

### 3. DIVERGE

Map materially different territories.

Possible dimensions:
- semantic/metaphoric source;
- lexical construction;
- descriptiveness/abstraction;
- identity mode;
- tonality/personality;
- sound/rhythm;
- narrative or phrase structure;
- emotional stance.

If the stakeholder supplies a vivid territory, treat it as one territory unless explicitly locked by the brief.

### 4. COUNTER-TERRITORY CHECK

Before convergence on an open brief:
- ask whether the active pool has become anchored to one user suggestion, metaphor, construction or tonality;
- if yes, create/preserve at least one materially different counter-territory whose mechanism could plausibly outperform it.

This is not an arbitrary name-count requirement.

### 5. GENERATE INTERNALLY

Generate a broad working pool.

Reject early only when clearly justified:
- unsupported credential/affiliation/outcome claims;
- direct confusing competitor copy;
- impossible platform constraints;
- obvious linguistic/harm hard fails;
- empty spelling gimmicks whose only value is apparent availability.

Do not perform exhaustive collision research on every raw idea before useful divergence exists.

Do not present the raw pool by default.

### 6. SCREEN

For serious candidates perform context-relevant checks.

#### Collision relevance triage

Classify observed external use:

- **T0 — semantic/search noise**: dictionary/descriptive/common phrase usage; not an identity collision by itself.
- **T1 — unrelated title/phrase identity**: song/book/article/creative title or remote-category use with low source-confusion plausibility.
- **T2 — adjacent identity signal**: active identity with some audience/channel overlap; materiality depends on context.
- **T3 — material identity collision**: active same/related-category or same-audience identity with plausible source confusion.
- **T4 — legal/high-risk escalation**: material trademark/legal uncertainty requiring specialist review.

Record the reasons. Do not infer legal safety from a low naming-collision tier.

#### Linguistic screening

Check:
- denotation/connotation;
- likely first meaning;
- morphology;
- pronunciation;
- homophones;
- plausible misspelling/transcription;
- word-boundary ambiguity;
- slang/phonetic overlap;
- cross-language risk within available competence.

### 7. FIRST-READ + ORAL/TYPED TEST

For each serious finalist:

#### Identity first read
Record:
- intended identity mode;
- likely first-read identity type;
- mismatch severity:
  `FATAL | MATERIAL | TOLERABLE | INTENTIONAL | UNRESOLVED`.

#### Spoken referral
Use realistic speech:
- “follow <name>”;
- “look up <name>”;
- equivalent project-appropriate phrases.

Then ask what a listener could plausibly type.

#### Lowercase handle form
Inspect:
- segmentation;
- repeated boundary letters;
- accidental alternate words;
- readability without capitalization/spaces.

#### Polysemy
State the likely first interpretation and meaningful alternate interpretations.

A finalist may remain viable with ambiguity, but a material unrecorded transcription/first-read defect blocks first-place recommendation.

### 8. NATURALNESS / CONTRIVANCE CRITIQUE

For serious finalists ask:

- Does it sound like something that can be comfortably said and remembered?
- Is its unusualness coherent with the approved identity, or visibly trying to signal unusualness?
- Does the name work before the rationale is explained?
- Would removing the clever backstory materially reduce its appeal?
- Is the construction repeated across the pool because the agent found a gimmick?

Classify weaknesses:
`FATAL | MATERIAL BUT TOLERABLE | REMEDIABLE | LOW IMPACT | UNRESOLVED`.

Do not turn this into a ban on experimentation.

### 9. CONSISTENCY CHECK

Before final ranking:
- list the main rule used to advance/penalize each finalist;
- compare near-equivalent cases;
- test whether the rule changes only because of irrelevant surface context;
- if treatment differs, state the decision-relevant reason.

Correct inconsistent scoring/rationale before handoff.

### 10. COMPARE

Compare finalists criterion-by-criterion.

Do not hide trade-offs behind averages.

A recommendation should explain:
- why the winner serves the brief;
- which weaknesses remain;
- why those weaknesses are tolerable relative to alternatives;
- which evidence is live vs unresolved.

### 11. HANDOFF

Return:
- decision state;
- naming brief;
- criteria/hard fails;
- territories explored;
- counter-territory result;
- finalists;
- screening ledger;
- trade-offs;
- recommendation;
- legal/localization/visual handoffs;
- unresolved evidence.

## Personal-brand handle mode

For a personal professional brand:

1. Determine whether the project wants:
   - name-led identity;
   - person-alias identity;
   - concept-led identity;
   - or open exploration across modes.
2. Do not assume including the person's name is inherently better.
3. Do not assume abstraction is inherently more creative.
4. If the desired mode is person-alias, test that the handle reads as a person rather than a room, company, project or job title.
5. If concept-led mode is intentional, place/project-like semantics may be acceptable; state the trade-off instead of misclassifying it.
6. Weight findability by actual discovery paths.
7. Prioritize durable interpretability over temporary niche keyword stuffing.
8. Never imply unsupported expertise/credential.
9. Treat exact Instagram/platform availability as live state only.

## Critical hard failures

Qualification-critical:
- fabricated positioning/customer evidence;
- unsupported professional credential/identity;
- guessed availability presented as fact;
- formal legal clearance/registrability certification;
- unsupported linguistic/cultural certification;
- unflagged material same/related-category confusion;
- criteria manipulation to rescue a favorite;
- fabricated consumer validation;
- unauthorized registration/purchase/publish action.

## Live research triggers

Retrieve current evidence when material for:
- competitor/category identities;
- domain/handle availability;
- platform username rules;
- trademark/status screening;
- current cultural/news associations;
- jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

Do not store volatile status as timeless professional knowledge.
