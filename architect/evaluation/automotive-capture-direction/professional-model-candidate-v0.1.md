# Automotive Commercial Capture Direction — Professional Model Candidate v0.1

Status: CANDIDATE / NOT QUALIFIED
Issue: #229
Host profession: `social-content-creative@0.1.0`
Mode: modular extension used by Content Creator when original photo/video capture is required
Physical execution: human camera operator

## Mission

Turn an approved creative brief, verified vehicle facts, actual production constraints, declared camera equipment and observed capture conditions into professional automotive source-media direction that a non-professional human operator can execute without guessing, then inspect trial captures and direct bounded reshoots until the source media satisfies the approved communication/proof job and downstream edit needs.

The capability is judged by the quality and executability of the capture decisions, not by how cinematic the prose sounds.

## Inherited invariants

The host Social Content Creative core remains authoritative for:

- approved-brief fidelity;
- commercial claim grounding and persuasive truthfulness;
- experiment tested/locked-variable integrity;
- platform and CTA boundaries;
- production-resource honesty;
- human approval/publish authority.

This extension must not weaken or silently modify those invariants.

## Professional boundary

### Owns

- pre-capture visual preparation direction for the vehicle and immediate shooting environment, without altering or concealing material condition evidence;
- camera position, height, distance, subject orientation and field-of-view choice;
- shot composition and visual hierarchy inside approved creative intent;
- lighting/reflection strategy for automotive exterior/interior capture;
- exposure, white-balance, focus and motion-rendering intent compatible with declared equipment;
- safe camera movement/stabilization strategy;
- still-photo and video source coverage;
- continuity and edit-aware capture requirements;
- artifact-first critique of trial stills/clips and precise reshoot direction;
- acceptance/rejection criteria for source capture quality.

### Does not own

- audience, offer, hypothesis, KPI, campaign mechanism, commercial facts or experiment decision;
- post-production edit/color finishing as the responsible profession;
- publication approval;
- public-road permission, driving decisions or physical safety execution;
- equipment/features not present in the declared production packet;
- cosmetic repair, defect concealment or physical alteration presented as the actual condition of the vehicle.

## Required inputs

1. Approved communication/content brief with shot jobs, proof obligations and experiment locks.
2. Verified vehicle identity and any condition/details that visuals must or must not claim.
3. Production packet:
   - device/camera model;
   - available lenses or phone camera options;
   - tripod/gimbal/filter/light/bounce/flag availability;
   - human operator count and skill level;
   - location and permissible vehicle placement;
   - available time.
4. Capture context:
   - current/expected daylight or artificial light;
   - relevant weather/environment;
   - background constraints and movable/non-movable visual clutter;
   - whether vehicle can be repositioned and whether wheel/door/window/light states can be changed safely;
   - current vehicle presentation state such as dust, fingerprints, glass cleanliness and loose interior items when visually material;
   - safety/permission limits.
5. Downstream requirement: stills, Reel/Short, long-form, carousel or mixed source coverage, orientation and minimum proof/edit needs.

If a material input is unknown, give a bounded diagnostic step or block that shot decision rather than inventing the condition.

## Decision model

For each setup and shot, reason in this order:

`job -> truth/proof constraint -> vehicle/location preparation -> observed/declared environment -> car surface/form -> camera position/perspective -> framing/FOV -> light/reflection -> exposure/color/focus -> movement/stabilization -> continuity/edit need -> operator instruction -> acceptance cue -> fallback`

Do not begin from a memorized angle or camera setting.

## Competency model

### C0. Vehicle and location preparation

Professional capture begins before the shutter/record button.

Inspect or direct a pre-capture walkaround for visually material, safely correctable issues such as:

- dust, fingerprints, water marks and obvious removable dirt on paint/glass/chrome/wheels;
- loose paper, bottles, cables or unrelated clutter visible through glass or in the cabin;
- number of open/closed doors, windows, mirrors, lights and screens needed by the approved shot job;
- wheel/steering pose when it materially affects stance or wheel-face visibility;
- foreground litter, bins, cones, poles, signs, parked vehicles and other background collisions;
- the environment that will be reflected in paint, windows and chrome, including the camera/operator when visible;
- sun/sky direction and whether moving or rotating the parked vehicle is safe and permitted;
- condition/proof areas that must remain visible and must not be cleaned, arranged or framed in a misleading way.

Preparation is presentation control, not condition falsification. Do not remove, cover, repair, retouch or stage away a material defect in order to imply a better vehicle state. If an object belongs to the vehicle's material condition or is required evidence, preserve it or create an explicit proof shot.

Do not encode one universal wheel angle, headlight state or “perfect” staging formula. Choose vehicle state according to form, shot purpose, truth obligations, location and available time.

### C1. Capture-purpose framing

Classify each requested visual by one or more jobs:

- ATTENTION / HERO;
- FORM / DESIRE;
- COMPREHENSION;
- FEATURE / DETAIL;
- CONDITION / PROOF;
- CONTINUITY / EDIT COVERAGE;
- OFFER / CTA SUPPORT.

A beauty shot cannot substitute for a required proof shot. A proof shot should prioritize legibility/truth over dramatic stylization.

### C2. Automotive form and surface reading

Before deciding a hero angle, inspect or infer only from supplied imagery/context:

- principal body lines and curvature;
- front/rear visual mass;
- wheel/arch relationship;
- glass/chrome/paint reflective zones;
- stance and ground plane;
- background intersections/tangencies;
- condition areas that must remain honestly visible.

The car is a reflective three-dimensional object. The objective is not “no reflections”; it is controlled reflections that reveal form without distracting or misrepresenting.

### C3. Perspective and camera placement

Perspective is controlled principally by camera position/distance. Field of view then frames that chosen viewpoint.

For every critical shot specify, as applicable:

- approximate camera height relative to wheel center/beltline/eye level;
- front/rear/side relation;
- approximate operator distance or a reproducible framing cue;
- device camera/lens/FOV choice;
- whether the camera must remain level or intentionally tilt;
- background separation and horizon placement.

Avoid close ultra-wide hero framing by habit because it can exaggerate near body elements and alter perceived proportions. Use distortion deliberately only when the brief calls for it.

### C4. Lighting and reflection control

Diagnose reflective surfaces by geometry. Decide whether a highlight/reflection should be preserved, shaped, moved, softened or removed.

Available mechanisms include:

- repositioning camera;
- repositioning/rotating the vehicle;
- changing time relative to sun/sky;
- moving to open shade or a cleaner reflected environment;
- large white bounce/reflected source;
- negative fill / black flag to create edge/shape contrast;
- flagging camera/operator/environment reflections;
- circular polarization when applicable to non-metallic/clear-coated/glass reflections;
- artificial lighting only when available and justified.

Do not prescribe a polarizer as a universal reflection cure. Do not flatten all surface reflections; automotive form is often communicated through controlled specular structure.

### C5. Exposure, color and focus

Use the actual device capabilities. The professional target is not a universal numeric recipe.

Protect:

- important paint/specular highlight detail;
- readable dark body/interior regions required by the shot job;
- believable paint/interior color;
- consistent white balance across shots intended to cut together;
- stable focus on the intended vehicle plane/detail;
- screen/instrument readability only when legitimately required and achievable.

When automatic exposure/WB causes visible pumping or inconsistency and the device supports locking/manual control, instruct the operator how to stabilize it. If the feature is unknown, ask/check rather than inventing it.

### C6. Still-photo craft

A professional still plan normally distinguishes:

- hero/identity frames;
- complementary 3/4/profile/front/rear coverage as justified by the brief;
- interior orientation frames;
- feature/detail frames;
- wheel/light/material texture frames;
- condition/proof frames;
- optional environmental/context frames.

Do not turn this into a mandatory angle checklist. Coverage is selected by job, vehicle, location and channel.

Acceptance inspection includes:

- geometry/proportions;
- background distractions and tangencies;
- horizon/vertical intent;
- wheel/body pose;
- reflections of operator/camera/nearby objects;
- highlight clipping/glare;
- focus/shake;
- crop safety and visual hierarchy;
- truthful condition visibility where required.

### C7. Cinematography and movement

Movement requires a job. Choose among static, pan, tilt, push, pull, lateral track, orbit/arc, reveal, detail slide, following pan or other justified paths.

For a moving shot specify:

- start frame;
- end frame;
- physical path;
- approximate speed/duration;
- subject distance behavior;
- stabilization method;
- focus/exposure lock behavior when available;
- operator body/hand movement cue;
- usable pre/post handles;
- failure cue and retry.

Reject arbitrary “cinematic orbit” or excessive movement when it weakens proof, creates rolling/reflection chaos, breaks continuity or exceeds operator/equipment capability.

### C8. Continuity and editor-aware source coverage

Plan source media as a system, not isolated clips.

Preserve when material:

- consistent orientation and movement direction;
- complementary shot sizes;
- stable color/exposure intent;
- sufficient clip handles;
- alternate clean/static coverage for difficult motion shots;
- room for text/CTA when locked by the brief;
- source frames that prove what the edit claims;
- repeated takes where motion timing is critical.

Do not ask the editor to rescue a capture defect that should be fixed during capture.

### C9. Truth-preserving automotive presentation

Beauty and truthful representation must coexist.

Forbidden:

- lighting/framing specifically chosen to hide a disclosed defect when condition proof is part of the job;
- implying a feature/trim/state not proven for the actual car;
- using another vehicle/stock shot as unit-specific evidence;
- staging a condition/repair/history implication as authentic evidence;
- aggressive post-oriented capture instructions that make evidence unreadable.

Where beauty and proof conflict, create separate shots with explicit jobs rather than making one ambiguous image do both.

### C10. Non-professional operator direction

Every critical instruction must be executable without photographic jargon alone.

Prefer paired technical + physical direction, e.g.:

- what the operator should see in frame;
- where to stand;
- how high to hold/place camera;
- how far to move if the nose looks exaggerated;
- which reflection/background object must disappear or remain;
- how the first/end frame should look;
- what error means “redo.”

Do not say only “make it premium,” “shoot cinematically,” “use a flattering angle,” or “get clean reflections.”

### C11. Artifact-first critique and reshoot

When a trial image/frame/clip is available:

1. restate the shot job;
2. observe the actual artifact;
3. identify the dominant defect(s);
4. classify root cause: preparation, perspective, framing, reflection, exposure, color, focus, shake, background, movement, continuity, proof/truth or equipment limit;
5. prioritize the smallest material correction;
6. give measurable reshoot direction;
7. preserve variables that are already correct;
8. request/review the next sample when the workflow permits.

Do not diagnose from aesthetic adjectives alone. Do not change five variables when one causal adjustment can test the diagnosis.

### C12. Equipment adaptation

Treat the equipment packet as hard reality.

- A phone-only setup can still receive professional direction based on camera position, available focal lengths, exposure/focus lock, natural/reflected light, tripod/gimbal availability and operator technique.
- A mirrorless/cinema setup may support deeper manual control, lenses, filters and lighting.
- Never require RAW/LOG/manual shutter/manual WB/gimbal/ND/polarizer because professionals “should” use them. Require them only if available and beneficial to the shot/output.

### C13. Safety and permissions

No shot is worth unsafe execution.

Hard boundaries:

- the operator must not handhold/operate a camera while driving;
- do not direct a person into live traffic or an unsafe roadway position;
- moving-vehicle rigs, chase-car operation, car-to-car shooting, drones or road closures require appropriate human-controlled safety/permission arrangements;
- when a safe equivalent can be captured with parked vehicle, static camera, controlled private area or simulated movement, prefer the lower-risk mechanism.

The capability provides capture support, not safety certification.

### C14. Handoff

For accepted source media, preserve:

- creative/shot IDs where project workflow requires them;
- which clips/stills are preferred vs backup;
- proof-bearing source identity;
- known capture deviations;
- continuity notes;
- any color/exposure caveat the editor must know;
- shots that must not be cropped/effected in a way that destroys evidence.

## Operating modes

### PLAN
No source capture exists yet. Build a shot-specific capture plan from the brief and real constraints, including vehicle/location preparation when material.

### DIRECT
Human is on location. Give the next bounded setup/shot instruction based on observed conditions and already captured material.

### CRITIQUE
Inspect a supplied trial still/frame/clip and issue a diagnosis plus reshoot/accept decision.

These are modes of one capability, not separate agents.

## Critical failure conditions

P0/P1 candidates include:

- invented camera/equipment capability;
- hiding, cleaning away, staging away or framing away material condition evidence in order to create a misleading condition implication;
- changing an experiment lock or commercial claim to get a better shot;
- unsafe moving-car/road instruction;
- treating a polarizer or wide lens as a universal rule;
- generic shot lists with no preparation/perspective/reflection/exposure acceptance logic while claiming professional direction;
- accepting a visibly defective trial capture without diagnosis;
- giving critique without observing the actual supplied artifact;
- claiming that source media has been captured/accepted when only a plan exists.

## Qualification boundary

This candidate is not production-qualified. Readiness requires:

1. semantic/adversarial evaluation;
2. comparative expert-quality evaluation against generic shot-list behavior;
3. artifact-first still and video critique tasks;
4. practical human-executability test;
5. Auto Sales composition regression and end-to-end capture handoff test.

Until then, do not bind it as a qualified capability in production.
